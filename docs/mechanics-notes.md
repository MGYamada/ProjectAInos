# Mechanics Formalization Notes

These notes record mathematical distinctions and modeling decisions made explicit by the Lean development, following the [thermodynamics notes](thermodynamics-notes.md). Each entry states its effect on the interface and its proof status. These are clarifications of the formal model, not claims of new physical results.

The [research plan](cinfty-mechanics.md) preserves the long-term programme. The [mechanics guide](mechanics.md) states the implemented endpoint and its hypotheses. Add future observations here with proof links; keep unresolved claims explicit.

## M01. Fixed endpoints are weaker than interior compact support

**Observation.** A fixed-endpoint variation satisfies `η(a)=η(b)=0`, but may have nonzero derivatives at both ends and may be nonzero arbitrarily close to them. For `a < b`, the scalar variation `η(t)=(t−a)(b−t)` has `η'(a)=b−a ≠ 0`.

**Interface decision.** `FixedEndpointVariation` requires smoothness and the two value equations, with no support condition. The previous common theorem for compactly supported interior variations alone would not cover this full class. The new common action-derivative theorem uses compactness of the base interval. Integration by parts retains the momentum pairing until the endpoint values cancel it.

**Proof status.** `polynomial_fixedEndpointVariation` and `polynomial_variation_left_derivative` check this example. `firstVariation_fixedEndpoints` covers the full variation class. Compactly supported interior tests are used only to infer the differential equation in the forward implication; the converse covers every fixed-endpoint variation. [Examples](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Examples.lean) · [First variation](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/FirstVariation.lean) · [Euler–Lagrange](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/EulerLagrange.lean)

## M02. The action derivative needs analytic justification

**Observation.** The identity for the pointwise derivative of `L(t,qε,qε')` does not, by itself, justify differentiating its integral. On a finite interval, joint C² regularity bounds that derivative on a compact product of parameter and time intervals. The resulting constant bound is integrable for restricted Lebesgue measure.

**Interface decision.** `hasDerivAt_action_restrict_compact` proves this analytic step in the common layer. The mechanics endpoint consequently has no assumed first-variation formula, no assumed fundamental lemma, and no independent domination or integrability premise. The theorem still explicitly assumes C² regularity of the ambient Lagrangian and path.

**Proof status.** The common domination argument and the actual `HasDerivAt` statement are proved. Reducing the hypotheses to functions defined only on a closed interval, or to weaker Sobolev regularity, remains future work. [Common regularity](../ProjectAInos/Variational/Regularity.lean) · [Mechanical action derivative](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/FirstVariation.lean)

## M03. Momentum and the residual are covectors

**Observation.** Both `∂L/∂q` and `∂L/∂v` naturally act on configuration vectors. Writing them as vectors would introduce an inner product and a dual-space identification that the variational argument does not require. In one-dimensional time, the common derivative `Dq : ℝ →L[ℝ] Q` becomes velocity by evaluation at `1`.

**Interface decision.** `force`, `momentum`, and `eulerLagrange` take values in `Q →L[ℝ] ℝ`. `positionVelocity` is an explicit continuous linear map connecting the shared first jet to the mechanical pair. The proof works for any real normed `Q`, and thus for every finite number of generalized coordinates, without a metric on `Q`.

**Proof status.** The jet identification, fibre derivative decomposition and covector-valued endpoint formula are proved. Coordinate transformations and cotangent bundles on a nonlinear configuration manifold are not yet implemented. [Basic interfaces](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Basic.lean) · [Covector integration by parts](../ProjectAInos/Variational/IntegrationByParts.lean)

## M04. A nondegenerate interval is necessary to detect an equation

**Observation.** When `a = b`, the action is identically zero for all paths. Stationarity then supplies no differential information. Also, the measure-theoretic fundamental lemma initially gives an almost-everywhere equation; it becomes pointwise because the residual is continuous and Lebesgue measure is positive on nonempty open intervals.

**Interface decision.** The equation-detecting equivalence requires `a < b` and states the equation on `(a,b)`. The first-variation identity itself retains oriented interval integrals. Changing between `[a,b]`, `(a,b]`, and `(a,b)` in scalar integrals uses the fact that endpoints have zero measure; it does not silently change the admissible endpoint values of a path.

**Proof status.** `stationary_self` proves the zero-length case, while `stationary_iff_eulerLagrange` contains the fundamental-lemma and continuity steps. No theorem about natural boundary conditions or free endpoints is asserted. [Euler–Lagrange](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/EulerLagrange.lean) · [Examples](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Examples.lean)

## M05. Stationarity, regularity of the Lagrangian, and minimality differ

**Observation.** Deriving Euler–Lagrange from first-order stationarity requires neither a positive velocity Hessian nor an invertible one. Invertibility is needed for later steps such as solving the equations for accelerations or constructing a Legendre equivalence. First-order stationarity alone makes no assertion of local or global minimality.

**Interface decision.** `Stationary` is defined by the actual parameter derivative, independently of the field equation. The equivalence assumes joint C² regularity but no Hessian condition. For the natural Lagrangian, Newton's equation is retained as `m q'' = −V'(q)`; this statement makes sense even at zero mass. Existence, uniqueness and minimality of trajectories are separate targets.

**Proof status.** The first-order equivalence and `stationary_natural_iff` are proved. No second-variation test, Legendre theorem or existence theorem is claimed. [Definitions](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Basic.lean) · [Natural Lagrangian](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Examples.lean)

## M06. The concrete smooth model does not settle arbitrary coefficient rings

**Observation.** A real smooth operation supplies an actual C∞ observable, and evaluation at `(t,q,v)` preserves all smooth substitutions. This connects the common algebraic API to the analytic action. It does not establish a fundamental lemma with coefficients in an arbitrary quotient C∞-ring, the unresolved issue noted in the original manuscript.

**Interface decision.** `ofSmoothOperation` uses the existing `SmoothRingHom` evaluation, derives joint C∞ regularity, and then invokes the real analytic theorem. The algebraic theory is not duplicated inside Mechanics. The analytic jet remains the pair of a field and its Fréchet derivative; it is not presented as a constructed abstract jet ring.

**Proof status.** `ofSmoothOperation_op` and `stationary_ofSmoothOperation_iff` prove the concrete bridge. Abstract quotients, dual numbers, universal smooth differentials, jet rings, and the coefficient-valued fundamental lemma remain research targets. [Smooth Lagrangians](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/SmoothLagrangian.lean) · [Original critical-subfunctor discussion](cinfty-mechanics.md#rem:fundamental)

## M07. A boundary term shifts momentum and preserves the equations

For $F(t,q)$, the action shift is $F(b,q(b))-F(a,q(a))$. Every path with the same endpoints receives the same shift, so the action differences between such paths are preserved. Along a fixed-endpoint variation, the shift is constant even when the endpoint velocities vary. The action identity and stationarity equivalence use C¹ regularity of $F$ and the path and continuity of $L$.

The velocity derivative of the added density is $\partial_q F$, so the momentum covector shifts by that amount. Along the path, the derivative of this shift cancels the extra position derivative by symmetry of $d^2F$. This proves pointwise equality of the Euler–Lagrange residuals with C² $F$, $L$, and $q$. At that regularity, the added density is guaranteed to be C¹, which is why the proof computes the cancellation directly. The momentum shift also identifies the correction to track when a symmetry changes a Lagrangian by a total derivative in the planned Noether development. [Lean proofs](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/TotalTimeDerivative.lean) · [Noether target](cinfty-mechanics.md#prop:noether-jet)

## M08. The chain rule is the only law of a C∞-derivation

**Observation.** For a C∞-ring `A` and an `A`-module `M`, a map `d : A → M` satisfying the chain rule for every finite smooth operation is automatically additive (binary addition), satisfies the Leibniz rule (multiplication), vanishes on real constants (nullary operations), and is real linear. The chain rule also gives identities that ordinary derivations of the underlying real algebra need not satisfy, such as `d (exp a) = exp a • d a`.

**Interface decision.** `SmoothDerivation A M` has the chain rule as its single field. `map_add`, `map_mul`, `map_algebraMap` and `map_smul` are theorems, so no redundant law has to be proved when a derivation is constructed. `toDerivation` produces Mathlib's `Derivation ℝ A M`. It needs `[Module ℝ M] [IsScalarTower ℝ A M]` because the definition assumes only an `A`-module; the constant-multiple law `map_algebraMap_mul` holds without a real action on `M`.

**Proof status.** The derived laws, `map_exp` and `toDerivation` are proved. The manuscript's counterexample showing that the converse fails (Kähler differentials of `C∞(ℝ)`) is not formalized. [Derivations](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/Derivation.lean)

## M09. Dual numbers need partial derivatives as operations

**Observation.** The smooth structure on `A[ε]` is `Φ_f(c + ε b) = Φ_f(c) + ε Σᵢ Φ_{∂ᵢf}(c) bᵢ`. Its substitution law is the multivariable chain rule, read as an identity between operations. A derivative evaluated at a real point, which was all the algebra layer had (`directionalEvaluation`), cannot state this law for an abstract ring: `∂ᵢf` has to be an operation that the ring can interpret.

**Interface decision.** `SmoothOperation.partialDeriv` returns an operation, and `partialDeriv_comp` is the chain rule in that form. The instance is placed on Mathlib's `DualNumber A`, so the ring and real-algebra structures are Mathlib's and no second ring instance exists. The general square-zero extension `A ⊕ M` is deferred: Mathlib's `TrivSqZeroExt` commutative-ring instance needs `Module Aᵐᵒᵖ M` and `IsCentralScalar A M`, which a consumer holding only an `A`-module would have to supply. For `M = A` they are automatic.

**Proof status.** The `SmoothRing (DualNumber A)` instance, the projection `fstHom`, the zero section `inlHom` and the nonpolynomial check `exp_inl_add_inr` are proved. [Partial derivatives](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/PartialDerivative.lean) · [Dual numbers](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/DualNumber.lean)

## M10. Tier A statements still rest on calculus of the operations, including (S)

**Observation.** The manuscript labels the dual-number structure (Example 2(c)) and the Lie algebra of vector fields (Definition 17) as tier A, "algebra over an abstract C∞-ring, no analysis", and says that milestones M1 and M2 depend on no analytic lemma except (H). The statements are indeed about an abstract ring, but their proofs use analytic facts about the operations `ℝⁿ → ℝ` themselves. The dual-number structure uses the chain rule. Closure of vector fields under the commutator uses the symmetry of second partial derivatives, the interface lemma (S): in `ξ(η(Φ_f(a))) − η(ξ(Φ_f(a)))` the second-order terms `Σ_{i,j} Φ_{∂ⱼ∂ᵢf}(a) (ξ(aⱼ) η(aᵢ) − η(aⱼ) ξ(aᵢ))` cancel only because `∂ⱼ∂ᵢf = ∂ᵢ∂ⱼf`.

**Interface decision.** The calculus facts are isolated in one module as identities between operations: `partialDeriv_comp` and `partialDeriv_comm`. Algebra over an abstract ring cites these identities and uses no analysis of its own. `commutator_chain` is the single place where (S) enters the theory of derivations.

**Proof status.** Both identities and the Lie-ring instance on `SmoothDerivation A A` are proved. The tier labels in the manuscript are unchanged; the accurate reading is that M2 depends on (S) through M1 as well as on (H) for quotients. [Partial derivatives](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/PartialDerivative.lean) · [Derivations](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/Derivation.lean)

## M11. Derivations of the free ring are free without constructing Ω

**Observation.** Proposition 16(a) says the cotangent module of `C∞(ℝⁿ)` is free on `dx₁, …, dxₙ`. The same content can be stated without the universal module: derivations of `C∞(ℝⁿ)` into any module correspond to arbitrary `n`-tuples of values on the coordinates. One direction is a single use of the chain rule, because every `f` equals `Φ_f(x₁, …, xₙ)`.

**Interface decision.** `apply_eq_sum_partialDeriv` and `equivCoordinates` state this Hom form. The coordinate fields `partialDerivation i` give a nondegenerate instance of `SmoothDerivation`, and `lie_partialDerivation_proj_smul` (`[∂ᵢ, xⱼ ∂ₖ] = δᵢⱼ ∂ₖ`) shows that the bracket is not trivial. These statements use the default `SmoothRing` instance on `SmoothOperation n`, whose operation is substitution; the lower-priority manifold instance on the same type agrees with it only after unfolding.

**Proof status.** The classification is proved as a bijection; its linearity is not packaged. The universal cotangent module `Ω_A`, its universal property, and Proposition 16(b) and (c) are not constructed. [Derivations](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/Derivation.lean)

## M12. Infinitesimal automorphisms are stated over the same ring

**Observation.** Proposition 18 identifies vector fields with morphisms `A → A[ε]` that reduce to the identity at `ε = 0`. Proposition 12 is more general: a `B`-point of the tangent functor is a morphism `φ : A → B` with a derivation along `φ`. A derivation along `φ` takes values in `B` regarded as an `A`-module through `φ`, which needs a module structure that depends on a morphism.

**Interface decision.** `equivInfinitesimalAut` uses the subtype of morphisms `φ` with `(φ a).fst = a` for all `a`; `fstHom_comp_toInfinitesimalAut` gives the equivalent form `fstHom ∘ φ = id`. Derivations along a morphism are left to the stage that introduces the tangent functor, so that the module structure through `φ` is designed once.

**Proof status.** Proposition 18 is proved. Propositions 12 and 13 and the tangent functor are not implemented. [Derivations](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/Derivation.lean)

## M13. A Poisson bracket cannot reuse the bracket notation of a ring

**Observation.** The manuscript's sketch declares `class PoissonCinftyRing (P) extends CinftyRing P, Bracket P P`. Mathlib already gives every ring the instance `Ring.instBracket`, the commutator `⁅x, y⁆ = x * y - y * x`. On a commutative ring this commutator is identically zero. Extending `Bracket P P` would therefore install a second instance on the same type, and `⁅f, g⁆` could resolve to the zero commutator.

**Interface decision.** `PoissonStructure P` is a structure with a field `bracket`, written `π.bracket f g`, and no notation. A structure also allows several Poisson structures on one ring, which a class would not: the canonical bracket and a Lie–Poisson bracket can live on the same free ring. The problem does not arise for vector fields: `SmoothDerivation A A` is not a ring, so its `Bracket` instance is the only one, and `hamiltonian_bracket` uses it on the right-hand side.

**Proof status.** Implemented in the second half of milestone M2. [Poisson structures](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/Poisson/Basic.lean)

## M14. The standard jet ring is the free ring on time and the jet coordinates

**Observation.** The standard jet model `Jₙ` is the ring of smooth functions of `t` and finitely many of the variables `qᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾`. `FreeSmoothRing ι` is already the ring of smooth functions on real assignments to `ι` with finite coordinate dependence, so `ι = Unit ⊕ (Fin n × ℕ)` gives the same carrier together with its universal property.

**Interface decision.** `StandardJet ι` is `FreeSmoothRing (Option (ι × ℕ))`, with `none` for time and `some (i, k)` for `qᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾`. The partial derivatives it needs were first expected to require calculus on the free ring; M15 records why they do not.

**Proof status.** Implemented: `StandardJet.jetRing` is a `JetRing`, and it landed together with the abstract structure so that the structure has a nondegenerate instance. The universal property of the standard jet ring among jet rings is not stated. [Free rings](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/Free.lean) · [Standard jet ring](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Jet/Standard.lean)

## M15. Vector fields on a free ring come from the dual numbers, without analysis

**Observation.** A vector field on the free C∞-ring should exist for every choice of values on the generators. Proving this by differentiating cylinder functions would need calculus on an infinite product and a proof that the result is again a cylinder function. Neither is necessary. The universal property of the free ring gives a morphism into its dual numbers that sends each generator `x` to `x + ε w(x)`; this morphism reduces to the identity at `ε = 0`, and by Proposition 18 its infinitesimal part is a derivation. Uniqueness is the chain rule applied to a presentation. Two coordinate vector fields commute because their commutator is a derivation that vanishes on every generator.

**Interface decision.** `FreeSmoothRing.derivation`, `derivation_ext` and `partialDerivation_comm` are stated for the free ring on any set of generators. The standard jet ring takes its `∂ₜ` and `∂ᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾` from them. The manuscript lists symmetry of second derivatives as the analytic input of the standard model; in Lean that input is used once, in the Lie-ring structure of derivations (M10), and the model itself is algebra.

**Proof status.** Proved. This construction gives vector fields with values in the ring itself. Derivations of a free ring into an arbitrary module would need the square-zero extension deferred in M09. [Derivations of free rings](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/FreeDerivation.lean)

## M16. A jet ring has no time coordinate, and boosts need one

**Observation.** Definition 32 gives a jet ring the vector field `∂ₜ` but no element `t`. The energy, momentum and angular-momentum laws never mention time. The boost of Corollary 38 has `ξ = t u`, so the centre-of-mass integral `u · (t P − Σ m q)` cannot even be written without a time coordinate.

**Interface decision.** `JetRing` follows Definition 32 and has no time field. `conserved_boost` takes an element `τ` with `∂ₜτ = 1` and `∂ᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾τ = 0` as explicit hypotheses and proves `Dτ = 1` from them. In the standard jet ring these hold for `StandardJet.time`.

**Proof status.** Proved, with the hypotheses instantiated in the standard model (`conserved_boost_free`). [Conservation laws](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Jet/Conservation.lean)

## M17. The Noether identity holds for every variation

**Observation.** Proposition 37 assumes that the components `ξᵢ` have order at most zero. The identity `pr ξ(L) = Σᵢ ξᵢ Eᵢ(L) + D(Σᵢ ξᵢ pᵢ)` for a first-order Lagrangian is only the Leibniz rule and holds for every `ξ`. The order restriction matters when `pr ξ` is read as the prolongation of a point transformation; the algebra does not use it.

**Interface decision.** `prolong` is defined for arbitrary `ξ` as `Σₖ Σᵢ (Dᵏ ξᵢ) ∂ᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾`, and `prolong_eq_euler_add_totalDeriv` has no hypothesis on `ξ`. The Euler operator and `euler_totalDeriv` are likewise stated for elements of every order, which covers the second-order Euler operator of experiment E9b.

**Proof status.** Proved. That `pr ξ` commutes with `D` for evolutionary `ξ` is not stated. [Noether identity](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Jet/Noether.lean)

## M18. Coordinates are indexed by a finite type, and the cross product comes from Mathlib

**Observation.** The manuscript indexes coordinates by `1, …, n`. Particles in space are naturally indexed by pairs (particle, component), and re-indexing them as `Fin (3 N)` would hide the structure that angular momentum uses. Mathlib's cross product `⨯₃` is defined on `Fin 3 → R` for every commutative ring `R`, so it applies to vectors whose entries are elements of a jet ring.

**Interface decision.** `JetRing ι J` takes any index type, with `[Fintype ι]` where sums occur. Angular momentum uses `ι = κ × Fin 3`: the variation is `n ⨯₃ qₐ`, the kinetic term vanishes by `(n × v) · v = 0`, and `axis_cross_dotProduct` rewrites the charge as `n · (q × p)`. Mathlib provides the Lie ring of the cross product only as a definition, `Cross.lieRing`, because `Fin 3 → R` is already a ring under componentwise multiplication and carries the commutator bracket. This is the same clash as in M13, so the rigid-body stage should use `⨯₃` directly and not bracket notation.

**Proof status.** `conserved_angularMomentum` and `conserved_linear` are proved. The rigid body of M24 uses `⨯₃` in the same way. The `so(3)` momentum map of the row for §9 is not formalized.

## M19. Conservation in a jet ring is ideal membership, not constancy along a path

**Observation.** In a jet ring, "conserved" means that the total derivative lies in the Euler–Lagrange ideal. This is a statement about elements of a ring. It becomes "constant along every solution" only after a path is prolonged to a morphism out of the jet ring that kills the Euler–Lagrange ideal and turns `D` into `d/dt`. For a first-order Lagrangian the energy identity involves the second derivative of the path, so that bridge needs at least `C²` paths, and the full ideal needs all derivatives.

**Interface decision.** `Conserved L F` is defined as `D F ∈ eulerLagrangeIdeal L`, and the ideal is proved stable under `D`. Nothing in `Mechanics/Jet` mentions a path. The real analytic statements (`Mechanics.stationary_iff_eulerLagrange` and its relatives) remain separate.

**Proof status.** The algebraic laws are proved, and the ledger rows for §§6–9 are *proved* for their stated main target, the jet-ring identity. The bridge to smooth trajectories is M29. [Euler operators](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Jet/Euler.lean)

## M20. Bilinearity and the Leibniz rule of a Poisson bracket are theorems

**Observation.** Definition 20 asks for an `ℝ`-bilinear bracket with antisymmetry (P1), the Jacobi identity (P2) and the chain rule in the first argument (P3). Bilinearity is redundant. The chain rule applied to the addition and multiplication operations gives additivity and the Leibniz rule in the first argument, applied to a constant operation it gives `{r, h} = 0`, and antisymmetry transports all three to the second argument. This is the same reduction as for derivations (M08), because (P3) says that `{·, h}` is a C∞-derivation.

**Interface decision.** `AlmostPoissonStructure` has the fields `bracket`, `antisymm` and `chain`; `PoissonStructure` adds `jacobi`. The lemmas `bracket_add_left`, `bracket_mul_left`, `bracket_algebraMap_left`, `bracket_smul_left` and their right-hand versions are derived through `hamiltonian h : SmoothDerivation P P`.

**Proof status.** Proved. `checks/PoissonAPI.lean` pins the derived laws. [Poisson structures](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/Poisson/Basic.lean)

## M21. On a free ring the Jacobi identity is a condition on generators

**Observation.** The manuscript marks the Jacobi identity of the canonical bracket and of a Lie–Poisson bracket as needing the symmetry of second derivatives. In Lean that input is used once and abstractly. For any almost-Poisson structure the Jacobiator `{{f, g}, h} + {{g, h}, f} + {{h, f}, g}` satisfies the chain rule in its first argument: the second-order terms of the three summands cancel by `partialDeriv_comm`. It is cyclic, so it is a derivation in each argument, and on a free ring it vanishes as soon as it vanishes on generators.

**Interface decision.** `jacobiator_op` is stated for every C∞-ring. `FreeSmoothRing.jacobiator_eq_zero` is stated for every set of generators, and `poissonOfCoordinates` takes the Jacobi identity on generators as its only hypothesis beyond skewness. For the canonical bracket the generator brackets are constants, so the hypothesis is `0 + 0 + 0 = 0` (`poissonOfConstants`); for the rigid body it is 27 cases decided by `fin_cases`.

**Proof status.** Proved. A general Lie–Poisson structure for a Lie algebra with a finite basis is not stated; `poissonOfCoordinates` reduces it to the Jacobi identity of the structure constants.

## M22. The coordinate bracket is defined for vector fields on any ring, then specialized

**Observation.** The first definition of the bracket `Σ_{a,b} ∂_a f ∂_b g w a b` was written directly on `FreeSmoothRing κ`. Its chain-rule proof timed out in definitional unfolding (`isDefEq`). The likely cause is that the free ring is a subalgebra of a function algebra, so each `ring` or `simp` step has to see its ring instances through that subtype; this was not profiled further. The same formula for a finite family of vector fields `D a` on an arbitrary C∞-ring and a skew family `w a b` elaborates at once, because the ring is a variable.

**Interface decision.** `AlmostPoissonStructure.ofDerivations D w hw` lives in the abstract file, and `FreeSmoothRing.almostPoisson w hw` is its instance with `D = partialDerivation`. The abstract form is also the mathematically natural one: it is the bracket of the bivector `Σ w a b · D_a ∧ D_b`. The same pattern was used for `FreeSmoothRing.derivation` (M15) and is the default for later constructions on free rings: prove over a variable ring, specialize last.

**Proof status.** Proved. `Poisson/Free.lean` compiles in about ten seconds.

## M23. Reduction, Hamilton–Jacobi and Maupertuis are statements about ideals

**Observation.** Proposition 28 concludes that `N(I)/I` is a Poisson C∞-ring, which needs the quotient of a C∞-ring by an ordinary ideal and therefore Hadamard's lemma. Every other part of the proposition is free of quotients: the normalizer is a subalgebra closed under the bracket and under smooth operations, it contains a coisotropic ideal, and the bracket of two normalizer elements changes by an element of `I` when the arguments change by elements of `I`. Propositions 54 and 55 are of the same kind. Proposition 55 assumes that `u` is a unit, but the congruence `{g, H'} ≡ u {g, H}` modulo `(H − E)` holds for every `u`; the unit is needed only to conclude that the two ideals are equal, so that the roles of `H` and `H'` can be exchanged.

**Interface decision.** `normalizer I` is a `Subalgebra ℝ P`, with `bracket_mem_normalizer`, `op_mem_normalizer` and `bracket_sub_bracket_mem` as separate theorems. `hamiltonJacobi` and `maupertuis` are stated as ideal memberships, and `maupertuis` has no unit hypothesis. `bracket_mul_sub_algebraMap` records the exact identity behind it.

**Proof status.** Proved, except the quotient ring. Once quotients by ideals exist, `bracket_sub_bracket_mem` is the well-definedness statement they need. The row for §44 stays at *evidence* and the row for §47 at *informal*, because the Jacobi metric, the variational statement and Jacobi's theorem on complete integrals are not formalized. [Poisson ideals](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/Poisson/Ideal.lean)

## M24. The rigid body: sign, the gradient form, and total inverses

**Observation.** With `X_h = {·, h}` and `{q, p} = 1`, the body-frame angular momentum has `{Lᵢ, Lⱼ} = −εᵢⱼₖ Lₖ`, the opposite sign to the space-frame components `q × p`. With this sign the equation of motion for any Hamiltonian is `{Lₐ, H} = (L × ∇H)ₐ`, and the whole bracket is `{f, g} = −L · (∇f × ∇g)`. Euler's equations and the Casimir property of `|L|²` are then instances of two identities of the cross product, `∇H = Ω` and `L · (L × v) = 0`. The kinetic energy `Σ Lᵢ² / 2Iᵢ` divides by the moments of inertia. Lean's real inverse is total, and the identities hold for every `I : Fin 3 → ℝ`, including `I i = 0`, where the corresponding term of the energy is zero.

**Interface decision.** `RigidBody.structureMatrix` is the explicit skew matrix, so that the generator brackets reduce by `rfl` and case analysis. The general statements `poisson_bracket` and `poisson_L_bracket` are in vector form with `⨯₃`, and Euler's equations are stated both in vector form and by components. No positivity or nonvanishing of `I` is assumed, since no proof uses it. `L_ne_zero` evaluates at the point `(1, 1, 1)` through `FreeSmoothRing.lift` to show that the bracket is not the zero bracket.

**Proof status.** Proved: the row for §36 is *proved*. The heavy top is M27. The quotient by `|L|² − r²`, stability of the principal rotations (§37) and the link to the Lagrangian on `T(SO(3))` are not formalized. [Rigid body](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Poisson/RigidBody.lean)

## M25. The canonical phase space is all of ℝ²ⁿ

**Observation.** Example 22(a) takes `P = C∞(U × ℝⁿ)` for an open `U ⊆ ℝⁿ`. `PhaseSpace ι` is the free ring, which is the case `U = ℝⁿ`. Kepler's problem lives on `ℝ³ ∖ {0}`, where `1/r` is a smooth function; in the free ring `r` is not invertible and `1/r` does not exist. A bracket on an open set needs the bracket to extend to a localization: a derivation extends uniquely to a C∞-localization, so the extension should exist, but it is not proved.

## M26. A momentum map is a Poisson morphism out of a free ring, checked on generators

## M29. Prolongation turns ideal membership into constancy along a path

**Observation.** The bridge announced in M19 needs no new structure. The ring of smooth functions of time is the C∞-ring `SmoothOperation 1`, and `d/dt` is its coordinate vector field. ## M33. The two notions of solution agree because they share one smooth function

**Observation.** The real analytic model defines a solution by stationarity of the action and proves that it is the vanishing of the Euler–Lagrange residual `∂L/∂q − d/dt ∂L/∂q̇`, a covector along the path. The jet ring defines a solution by the prolongation annihilating `Eᵢ(L)`. Both are built from the same datum when the Lagrangian is `Φ_f(t, q, q̇)` for a smooth function `f` of `2n + 1` variables: `ofSmoothOperation f` evaluates `f` on real jets, and `firstOrder f` applies the operation `f` to the jet coordinates. The comparison is then a computation on each side that ends in the same real expression, `∂_{qᵢ}f − d/dt ∂_{q̇ᵢ}f` on the real jet `(t, γ(t), γ̇(t))`. On the jet side this is the chain rule of the coordinate vector fields and `prolongation_totalDeriv`. On the analytic side it is the Fréchet derivative of `f` composed with the affine map `(q, v) ↦ (t, q, v)`, and the rule for differentiating a covector-valued function applied to a fixed vector. A solution in the jet ring is a condition at all times, so the variational statement that matches it is stationarity on every interval; the converse direction uses the interval `(t − 1, t + 1)` around each time.

**Interface decision.** The arguments of `f` are indexed by `Fin (n + n + 1)` with `Fin.cons` and `Fin.append`, as `ofSmoothOperation` already does. `positionIndex`, `velocityIndex` and the case principle `index_cases` hide that encoding; all later statements use them. The comparison is stated at each time, `prolongation_euler_firstOrder_apply`, and the equivalences `isSolution_firstOrder_iff` and `isSolution_firstOrder_iff_stationary` follow. `energy_eq_of_stationary` has no jet ring in its statement: its hypotheses are `∂f/∂t = 0` and stationarity, and its conclusion is an equality of real numbers.

**Proof status.** Proved for smooth paths defined on all of `ℝ` and Lagrangians defined on all of `ℝ^{2n+1}`. The real analytic model itself covers `C²` paths on one interval; the jet-ring side cannot, because a morphism out of the jet ring needs all derivatives. Lagrangians on an open jet domain (`Mechanics.OpenDomain`) and higher-order Lagrangians are not compared. [Analytic comparison](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Jet/Analytic.lean)

## M34. The Coriolis and centrifugal terms need no hypothesis on the mass

**Observation.** With `H = |p|² / 2m + U − Ω · M` the velocity is `v = {q, H} = p / m − Ω × q`, and the acceleration `{v, H}` contains `(Ω × p) / m`. The textbook substitutes `p = m (v + Ω × q)`, which divides by `m`. The substitution is not needed: `p / m = v + Ω × q` is the definition of `v` read backwards, so `(Ω × p) / m = Ω × v + Ω × (Ω × q)` holds as an identity in the generators and in the constant `m⁻¹`, for every real `m`. The mass must be nonzero only in the energy form `m |v|² / 2 − m |Ω × q|² / 2 + U`, where `m · m⁻¹` occurs. The bracket with `Ω · M` is a general fact about vectors: `{xₐ, Ω · M} = (Ω × x)ₐ` for every `x` with `{Mₐ, x_b} = εₐ_bc x_c`, which gives the `q` and `p` equations at once.

**Interface decision.** `RotatingFrame.velocity` is defined with `m⁻¹`, and `canonical_velocity_hamiltonian` has no hypothesis on `m`; `hamiltonian_eq_velocity` takes `m ≠ 0`. The angular velocity is a real vector, embedded as constants of the phase space. The potential is any element with vanishing derivatives in the momenta. The Hamiltonian `H₀ − Ω · M` is the definition, as in the row for §39; it is not derived from a change of frame.

**Proof status.** Proved: the row for §39 is *proved*. ## M35. The angular velocity is a theorem about rotations, proved entry by entry

**Observation.** Landau–Lifshitz §31 obtains `v = V + Ω × r` from an infinitesimal rotation. The precise statement is about a family of orthogonal matrices: differentiating `R Rᵀ = 1` shows that `Ṙ Rᵀ` is skew-symmetric, a skew-symmetric matrix acts as `r ↦ Ω × r` for a unique `Ω`, and the velocity of the body point `c + R r₀` is `ċ + Ṙ r₀ = ċ + (Ṙ Rᵀ)(R r₀)`. Mathlib has no derivative rule for products of matrix-valued functions, and the normed structure on matrices is not a global instance. Neither is needed: with entrywise derivatives the product rule is the scalar one applied to each `Σₖ Rₐₖ R_bₖ`. Mathlib's commutator Lie structure on an associative ring is a local instance in this version, so the statement that `so(3) ≅ ℝ³` respects brackets is made in the Lie subalgebra `so (Fin 3) ℝ`, whose bracket is global.

**Interface decision.** Differentiability is assumed entry by entry, `∀ a b, HasDerivAt (fun s => R s a b) (R' a b) t`, and orthogonality as `R s * (R s)ᵀ = 1` for all `s`; no topology on matrices appears. `hat`, `vee` and `hat_cross` are stated over any commutative ring, and `hat_vee`, `hatEquiv` over the reals, where a skew-symmetric matrix has zero diagonal. "Independent of the origin" is `velocityField_change_of_origin`, stated for two descriptions of one velocity field, with uniqueness `velocityField_injective` as the content.

**Proof status.** Proved: the row for §31 is *proved*. Determinant one is not used, so the statements hold for all orthogonal matrices. [Angular velocity](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Rigid/AngularVelocity.lean)

## M36. The triangle inequality for the principal moments is a trace inequality

**Observation.** `I₁ + I₂ ≥ I₃` is usually read off the diagonal form of the inertia tensor. It follows without coordinates from `tr I − 2 n · I n = 2 Σₐ mₐ (rₐ · n)² ≥ 0` for every unit vector `n`: a principal moment is `n · I n` for its principal axis, and the trace is the sum of the three. Each ingredient is one identity of the cross product in Mathlib: `M = I Ω` is the expansion of `r × (Ω × r)`, and `Ω · I Ω = Σ m |Ω × r|²` with the Lagrange identity gives both non-negativity and the trace inequality. Mathlib's `PosSemidef` is phrased with finitely supported functions; the quadratic-form inequality `0 ≤ x · I x` is stated directly.

**Interface decision.** `inertia` is defined by its entries for a finite family of point masses, with masses and positions as functions on an arbitrary finite type. `principalMoments` and `principalAxes` are Mathlib's eigenvalues and eigenvector basis of a Hermitian matrix; existence of principal axes is the spectral theorem and is not reproved. The parallel-axis theorem is stated entrywise, with the centre of mass at the origin as the hypothesis `Σ m • r = 0`. Masses are real numbers, and non-negativity is a hypothesis only where it is used.

**Proof status.** Proved: the row for §32 is *proved* for point masses. Continuous mass distributions, the classification of tops, and the regular precession of §33 are not formalized; the row for §33 stays *informal* with `M = I Ω` and the kinetic energy proved. ## M37. Kepler's problem: adjoin `1/r` as a generator and keep the relation as an element

**Observation.** The manuscript remarks that the six identities of Proposition 69 are polynomial identities once `r` and `r⁻¹` are adjoined subject to `r² = q · q`. That remark is a construction. Take the free C∞-ring on `q`, `p` and one more generator `ρ`, with the canonical brackets and `{ρ, pⱼ} = −ρ³ qⱼ`, `{ρ, qⱼ} = 0`, which is what `1/|q|` satisfies. This is a Poisson structure, and `c = ρ² |q|² − 1` has `{pⱼ, c} = 2 ρ² qⱼ c` and vanishing brackets with `q` and `ρ`, so `(c)` is a Poisson ideal. Nothing has to be inverted or divided. Each identity then holds either exactly or up to an explicit multiple of `c`: `{Aₐ, H} = α ρ pₐ c`, `{Aₐ, A_b} = −(2mH + mαρ c) εₐ_bc M_c`, `|A|² = m²α² + 2mH|M|² + m²α² c`, while `{M, A}`, `A · M` and `A · q = |M|² − m α r` are exact with `r = ρ |q|²`. Stating the multiple is stronger than stating membership in the ideal, and it shows where `ρ² |q|² = 1` is used. A solution of Newton's equations with `R = 1/|Q|` kills `c`, and the equation for `R` need not be assumed: differentiating `R² |Q|² = 1` and using `Q̇ = P/m` gives `Ṙ = −R³ (Q · P)/m`. Both signs of `R` are allowed; the other sign is the repulsive field.

**Interface decision.** The identities are proved for a *Kepler frame* in an arbitrary Poisson C∞-ring: a triple `q`, `p`, `ρ` with the five bracket relations and no relation between `ρ` and `q`. The free ring with `ρ` adjoined is a model (`KeplerModel.isFrame`), and the same theorems will apply to `C∞(T*(ℝ³ ∖ 0))` once it exists, with `constraint = 0`. ## M39. An elastic collision is a vector with the length of the relative velocity

**Observation.** Landau–Lifshitz §17 derives the velocities after a collision in the centre-of-mass frame and remarks that the direction `n₀` is not determined by the conservation laws. Both statements are one equivalence: conservation of momentum and of kinetic energy holds if and only if the velocities afterwards are `V + (m₂/M) w` and `V − (m₁/M) w` for a vector `w` with `|w| = |v₁ − v₂|`. The proof needs one identity, `M (m₁ v₁² + m₂ v₂²) = (m₁ v₁ + m₂ v₂)² + m₁ m₂ (v₁ − v₂)²`, which has no denominators and holds for all masses. Applied before and after the collision it gives the length of the relative velocity, with `m₁ m₂ ≠ 0`; applied to the outgoing velocities it gives the converse, with `M ≠ 0`. The same identity divided by `2M` is the splitting of the kinetic energy of §13, and with `p₁ + p₂ = 0` it is the disintegration formula of §16. The angles of the textbook enter only through `n · n₀`, the cosine of the deflection in the centre-of-mass frame; the transferred energy is bounded because `n · n₀ ≥ −1` for unit vectors.

**Interface decision.** `IsElastic` is a structure with the two conservation laws as fields, and `isElastic_iff` is the characterization. `outgoingOne` and `outgoingTwo` are functions of the incoming velocities and of `w`, so the formulas for a target at rest are statements about `outgoingOne m₁ m₂ (v • n) 0 (v • n₀)`. Vectors are `Fin 3 → ℝ` with `⬝ᵥ`, as in the rest of the mechanics models, and unit vectors are given by `n ⬝ᵥ n = 1`. Hypotheses on the masses are exactly those used: `m₁ m₂ ≠ 0`, `m₁ + m₂ ≠ 0`, or positivity for the two inequalities. No angle and no trigonometric function appears.

**Proof status.** Proved: the rows for §16 and §17 are *proved*. ## M41. The general natural Lagrangian: where symmetry and the inverse are used

**Observation.** For `L = ½ Σ aᵢₖ vᵢ vₖ − U` with coefficients of order zero, the derivative of the kinetic form with respect to `vⱼ` is `Σᵢ aᵢⱼ vᵢ + Σₖ aⱼₖ vₖ`. Symmetry of `a` is used once, to write the momentum as `Σₖ aⱼₖ vₖ`; everything after that is the Leibniz rule and a reordering of finite sums. Lagrange's equations then contain the combination `∂ⱼaᵢₖ − ½ ∂ᵢaⱼₖ`, the Christoffel symbols of the first kind before symmetrization in `j, k`. The energy is `½ Σ a v v + U` because the kinetic form is homogeneous of degree two, which appears as `½ + ½ = 1` for the constant `½`. To write the energy in the momenta one needs `v = b p`, and only a left inverse `Σⱼ bᵢⱼ aⱼₗ = δᵢₗ` is used; positive definiteness of `a` plays no role in these identities. A co-ordinate on which a first-order Lagrangian does not depend has a conserved momentum, by `Eᵢ(L) = ∂ᵢL − D pᵢ`, with no hypothesis on the form of `L`.

**Interface decision.** `kineticForm a` and `metricLagrangian a U` take the coefficients as a function `ι → ι → J`, with order zero, symmetry and the inverse as hypotheses where they are used. The constant `½` is `algebraMap ℝ J (1 / 2)`, and the only fact about it is `algebraMap_half_add_half`. `metricLagrangian_diagonal` shows that the constant-mass `naturalLagrangian` is a special case, so the earlier conservation laws are not restated. Plane polar co-ordinates are the model with a kinetic form that is not constant; there `r` is a generator and need not be invertible, since no identity divides by it. The lemmas for the entries of a matrix literal are not simp lemmas: marking them simp rewrites `metric 1 1` before the lemma for its derivative can fire.

**Proof status.** Proved: the row for §5 is *proved*. The Legendre map as an isomorphism between velocity and momentum variables (Example 52, Theorem 51) is not formalized and is the target of the row for §40. Geodesics, the symmetrized Christoffel form of the equations, and constraints are not treated. ## M42. Eulerian angles are polynomial identities modulo three relations

**Observation.** Every formula of Landau–Lifshitz §35 is an identity between polynomials in the cosines and sines of the three angles and in the rates of the angles, modulo `cₖ² + sₖ² = 1`. No trigonometric function and no topology is needed to state them: the rotation is `R_z(φ) R_x(θ) R_z(ψ)` with entries in a commutative ring, and its derivative is the matrix obtained from the product rule with `∂R_z` and `∂R_x` written out. The angular velocity in the body frame is the vector of `Rᵀ Ṙ`, the one in the space frame is the vector of `Ṙ Rᵀ`, and they are related by `ω = R Ω`. Trigonometry and derivatives enter in one lemma, which says that for differentiable angles the entrywise derivative of the rotation is that matrix. The linear map from the rates to `Ω` has determinant `−sin θ`: the co-ordinates degenerate exactly at `θ = 0, π`, which is where the textbook's line of nodes is undefined.

**Interface decision.** `eulerMatrix`, `eulerMatrixDeriv`, `bodyAngularVelocity` and `spaceAngularVelocity` take the six elements `c1 s1 c2 s2 c3 s3` and the three rates as separate arguments, so that each matrix entry is a polynomial in named atoms. Each of the nine entries of `R Rᵀ = 1`, `Rᵀ Ṙ = hat Ω` and `Ṙ Rᵀ = hat ω` is closed by `linear_combination` against the three relations. The coefficients were computed by polynomial division with SymPy and are checked by Lean; they are not trusted. `eulerRotation` specializes to real angles and is a member of Mathlib's `specialOrthogonalGroup`. The product rule for matrices is proved entry by entry, as in M35.

**Proof status.** Proved: the row for §35 is *proved*, with "a chart on `SO(3)`" read as membership in `SO(3)` together with the Jacobian of determinant `−sin θ`. Not formalized: surjectivity of the angles onto `SO(3)`, the local inverse, the general (asymmetric) kinetic energy beyond its definition `½ Σ Iₖ Ωₖ²`, and the Lagrangian of the heavy symmetric top in Eulerian angles. ## M43. Two first integrals give the general solution of the oscillator

**Observation.** The statement "every solution of `ẍ + ω² x = 0` is `c₁ cos ωt + c₂ sin ωt`" is a uniqueness theorem for a differential equation, but it needs no general theory of differential equations. The two combinations `x cos ωt − (ẋ/ω) sin ωt` and `x sin ωt + (ẋ/ω) cos ωt` have zero derivative by a computation in the ring of smooth functions of time, so each is a constant, and `cos² + sin² = 1` recovers `x` from them. The only analytic input is the same as for every conservation law in this development: a function with zero derivative is constant. The constants are the position and `ẋ/ω` at time zero, so the same computation gives existence, uniqueness and the flow. The hypothesis `ω ≠ 0` is used to divide by `ω`; conservation of `ẋ² + ω² x²` holds without it. The amplitude and the phase are the modulus and the argument of the complex number `c₁ − i c₂`, and the phase is defined for `c₁ = c₂ = 0` as well, so the form `a cos (ωt + α)` needs no case distinction.

**Interface decision.** `IsHarmonic ω x` is the equation in `TimeFunction`, with the frequency as a real parameter and the constant `ω²` as `algebraMap ℝ TimeFunction (ω ^ 2)`. The Lagrangian and the Hamiltonian of the oscillator are the existing ones (`StandardJet.oscillatorPotential`, `PhaseSpace.kineticEnergy`, `PhaseSpace.oscillatorPotential`), with one stiffness `k` and masses `mᵢ`; the frequencies enter through the hypothesis `k = mᵢ ωᵢ²`, so no square root appears in the statements. The flow is the matrix `Oscillator.flowMatrix m ω t` on a pair `(q, p)`; its determinant one is the whole content of "canonical" in the phase plane. Identities between functions of time that contain real constants are proved pointwise after `TimeFunction.ext_apply_const`, where `field_simp` and `ring` apply. A practical point: `open scoped ContDiff` makes `ω` a reserved token, so a file that names a frequency `ω` must not open that scope.

**Proof status.** Proved, including the equivalence with the solutions of the Euler–Lagrange equations and with the trajectories of the Hamiltonian. Not formalized: the complex representation `x = Re (A e^{iωt})` of (21.12), and the flow of a general quadratic Hamiltonian as a one-parameter group (its vector field is linear, M61). [Free oscillations](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Oscillations/Harmonic.lean) · [The oscillator](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Oscillations/Oscillator.lean)

## M44. Linearization at an equilibrium is the vector field of the quadratic part

**Observation.** "Small oscillations" contains two different statements. The first is algebraic: at a critical point `x₀` of `H`, the matrix `∂_c {x_a, H}(x₀)` of the linearized vector field is the matrix of the Hamiltonian vector field of the quadratic part `½ Σ H_ab(x₀) ξ_a ξ_b`, which is exactly linear in the displacements. For the canonical bracket this is the symmetry of second partial derivatives and nothing else; the point need not even be critical for the identity of matrices, and criticality is what makes the constant curve a trajectory. The second statement is analytic: solutions of the non-linear equations that start near the equilibrium stay near the solutions of the linearized equations for a certain time. Landau–Lifshitz use the second without stating it. Only the first is formalized. A third point is that "equilibrium" has a meaning before any linearization: the constant curve is a trajectory, which is the vanishing of the brackets `{x_a, H}` at the point, because evaluating an observable along a curve at a time is evaluating it at the point of the curve.

**Interface decision.** `hessian`, `quadraticPart` and `linearization` are defined for a free C∞-ring on a finite index type, with the point as a function `x₀ : κ → ℝ` and evaluation at the point as `FreeSmoothRing.lift x₀`; `linearization` takes any Poisson structure, and the theorems are for the canonical bracket. A bracket whose values on generators are not constant (the rigid body, §37) has a further term in its linearization, which is not treated. The one-dimensional Hamiltonian is `½ b(q) p² + U(q)` with the inverse mass `b` as a smooth function, so that no division by a function is needed; Landau–Lifshitz's coefficient is `a = 1/b`. The frequency enters as a hypothesis `ω² = b(q₀) U''(q₀)`, and the square root appears in one corollary, under the hypotheses of a non-degenerate minimum and a positive mass. The pendulum is the instance that shows the hypotheses can be met. In proofs about `restPoint q₀`, the lemmas on the Hessian must be applied before `restPoint` is unfolded, since they are stated for the folded point.

**Proof status.** Proved for the canonical bracket, with the one-dimensional case and the pendulum. Not formalized: the comparison of non-linear and linearized solutions, the Legendre transformation from `½ a(q) q̇² − U(q)`, the linearization as a morphism out of the dual numbers (a curve with values in `TimeFunction[ε]`), and several degrees of freedom with the simultaneous diagonalization of §23. [Linearization](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Oscillations/Linearization.lean)

## M45. One substitution reduces the damped equation to three model equations

**Observation.** The three cases of §25 are one computation followed by three elementary uniqueness statements. With `y = e^{γt} x`, the equation `ẍ + 2γ ẋ + ω₀² x = 0` is equivalent to `ÿ = −(ω₀² − γ²) y`, an identity in the ring of smooth functions of time that uses only the derivative of the exponential and the fact that `e^{γt}` is invertible. The sign of `ω₀² − γ²` then selects `ÿ = −ω² y` (M43), `ÿ = 0`, or `ÿ = β² y`. Each is solved by exhibiting functions with zero derivative: `ẏ` and `y − ẏ(0) t` for the second, `(ẏ − β y) e^{βt}` and `(ẏ + β y) e^{−βt}` for the third. Since the trichotomy of real numbers covers all cases, uniqueness for given `x(0)` and `ẋ(0)` holds for all real `γ` and `ω₀`, including negative damping and `ω₀ = 0`; no Picard–Lindelöf theorem is used. The book's conditions `λ < ω₀`, `λ = ω₀`, `λ > ω₀` assume positive `λ` and `ω₀`; the statements here are in terms of `ω₀²` and `γ²` and need no sign.

**Interface decision.** `IsDamped γ ω₀ x` is the equation solved for `ẍ`, like `IsHarmonic`, and `isDamped_zero_iff` identifies the undamped case. The damping coefficient is `γ` because `λ` is a reserved word of Lean. The frequencies `ω` and `β` enter through hypotheses `ω² = ω₀² − γ²` and `β² = γ² − ω₀²`, so that the statements contain no square root; the square roots appear only inside the proof of `IsDamped.eq_of_initial`. The explicit solutions are stated pointwise in real functions (`Real.exp`, `Real.cos`), and existence is stated in the ring (`isDamped_expMul` for a root of the characteristic polynomial). The energy `ẋ² + ω₀² x²` is shown not to increase by a general lemma: a smooth function of time whose derivative is nowhere positive is antitone.

**Proof status.** Proved. Not formalized: that the solution tends to zero for `γ > 0` in the critical and strong cases (the weak case has the bound `|x| ≤ a e^{−γt}`), and the complex form of the characteristic roots. [Damped oscillations](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Oscillations/Damped.lean)

## M46. Friction is a vector field, not a Hamiltonian, and `≤ 0` is a statement at points

**Observation.** The motion with a frictional force linear in the velocities is the vector field `X = X_H − Σ fᵢ ∂/∂pᵢ` on the canonical phase space. The notion of trajectory used so far, `IsTrajectory π H c`, refers to a Hamiltonian; the notion that survives is that of an integral curve of a vector field, and a trajectory is the special case of the Hamiltonian vector field. The identity `X(H) = −2F` is algebra in the ring: `X_H(H) = {H, H} = 0`, and the frictional term gives `Σ fᵢ vᵢ = 2F`. It does not use the symmetry of `α`; only the symmetric part of `α` enters `F`, while the force uses all of `α`, so an antisymmetric part (a gyroscopic force) changes the motion and not the energy balance. The inequality `−2F ≤ 0` is not a statement in the ring, which has no order. It is the statement that the value of `F` at every point is non-negative, and it holds when the quadratic form of `α` is non-negative. Along an integral curve this gives a derivative that is nowhere positive, hence an energy that does not increase.

**Interface decision.** `IsIntegralCurve X c` is defined for a vector field on a free C∞-ring, and `IsIntegralCurve.timeDerivative_lift` is the same proof as for trajectories. The friction matrix is a `Matrix ι ι ℝ` with real entries; positive semidefiniteness is the explicit hypothesis `∀ v, 0 ≤ v ⬝ᵥ α *ᵥ v`, since Mathlib's `Matrix.PosSemidef` is phrased with finitely supported functions and adds a symmetry condition that is not needed. The velocities are `∂H/∂pᵢ`, so the definitions apply to every Hamiltonian, and the oscillator with a diagonal `α` is the instance that connects to `IsDamped`. A lemma `SmoothDerivation.sum_apply` for finite sums of derivations was added in this file and not in the shared algebra layer, to avoid rebuilding the whole library for one line.

**Proof status.** Proved in Hamiltonian form. Not formalized: the Lagrangian form `d/dt ∂L/∂ẋᵢ − ∂L/∂xᵢ = −∂F/∂ẋᵢ` of (25.12), that `X` does not preserve the bracket, the contraction of the phase volume, and the existence of integral curves for a general Hamiltonian. [The dissipative function](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Oscillations/Dissipation.lean)

## M47. The steady state is a limit, and the resonance curve is exact

**Observation.** Three statements of §26 are usually run together. (1) The solutions for a given force form an affine space over the solutions of the damped equation; this is linearity and needs no analysis. (2) "After a sufficient time only the forced oscillation remains" is a limit: every solution of the damped equation tends to zero when `γ > 0` and `ω₀ ≠ 0`. The hypothesis `ω₀ ≠ 0` is necessary, since for `ω₀ = 0` the constants are solutions. With (1) this gives that any two solutions for the same force approach each other, for every force, periodic or not: the motion forgets its initial conditions. (3) For a periodic force there is an explicit solution, and by (2) it is the common limit. The resonance curve of the book is an approximation near `Ω = ω₀` for small `γ`. The exact curve `m γ F² Ω² / ((ω₀² − Ω²)² + 4γ² Ω²)` has properties that need no approximation: its maximum is at `Ω = ω₀` exactly, with value `m F²/4γ`, and it takes half of that value exactly at `√(ω₀² + γ²) ± γ`, so its full width at half maximum is exactly `2γ`. The amplitude has its maximum elsewhere, at `Ω² = ω₀² − 2γ²`. The absorption is a mean over a period, and this is the first statement of the mechanics development whose content is an integral; pointwise, twice the dissipative function is the absorption plus a term of frequency `2Ω`.

**Interface decision.** `IsForced γ ω₀ g x` takes the force per unit mass as a smooth function of time. The frequency of the force is `Ω`, since `γ` is already the damping coefficient (M45). The steady state is defined by its two coefficients `steadyCos` and `steadySin`, which are the real and the imaginary part of the complex response; `steadyAmplitude` and `steadyPhase` are derived, and the phase is characterized by `b cos δ` and `b sin δ` and not by `tan δ`, which is undefined at `Ω = ω₀`. The hypothesis on the frequency is `responseDenominator γ ω₀ Ω ≠ 0`; for `γ ≠ 0` and `ω₀ ≠ 0` it holds for every `Ω`. The proof that the steady state is a solution uses the two linear equations satisfied by the coefficients and `linear_combination`; `field_simp` did not clear the denominator from the hypothesis that a sum of squares is non-zero. The absorption is defined as `m γ b² Ω²` and then proved to be the mean dissipated power, with Mathlib's interval integral.

**Proof status.** Proved. Not formalized: the Lorentzian approximation (26.5), (26.9) and the area (26.10) under it, which are asymptotic; the complex response function as a function of `Ω`; a force `F cos (Ωt + β)` with a phase, which follows by superposition with the `sin` force but is not stated; and the response to a general force (§22). ## M48. Duhamel's formula is the fundamental theorem of calculus for two first integrals

**Observation.** The two functions of M43 whose derivative vanishes for the free oscillator are, up to a factor `ω`, the real and the imaginary part of `(ẋ + iωx) e^{−iωt}`. With a force `g` their derivatives are `g cos ωt` and `−g sin ωt`: the same computation in the ring, with one more term. The fundamental theorem of calculus then gives both functions as integrals of the force, and the position, the velocity and the energy are algebraic consequences. Written with the factor `ω` and not `1/ω`, the two functions and their integral formulas hold for every `ω`, including `ω = 0`; only the formula for the position divides by `ω`. Landau–Lifshitz's complex variable `ξ = ẋ + iωx` is this pair, and no complex number is needed. The formula also runs backwards: `(1/ω)(sin ωt ∫₀ᵗ g cos − cos ωt ∫₀ᵗ g sin)` is a smooth function of time, because a primitive of a smooth function is smooth, and a computation in the ring shows that it is a solution. So existence for every smooth force is proved here, not assumed; for the damped equation and for the oscillator, existence was already explicit. The energy transfer (22.12) of the book is for a force acting during all time. The statement proved is the exact formula at a finite time `T`; the limit needs improper integrals and hypotheses on the force.

**Interface decision.** `Mechanics/TimePrimitive` holds the two facts of calculus that are used: the increment of a function of time is the integral of its derivative, and `TimeFunction.primitive`. It opens the scope `ContDiff`, so it must not name a frequency `ω` (M43); the oscillation files do not open that scope. Integrals are Mathlib's interval integrals of the real functions `s ↦ g (fun _ => s) * Real.cos (ω * s)`. The notation `∫ s in a..b, f s` extends over `+` and `−` to its right, so an integral that is not the last term of an expression must be put in parentheses; without them the statement is a different one and still type-checks. The equation is `IsForced 0 ω g x`, so that superposition, uniqueness and the steady state of M47 apply without change. At resonance, unboundedness is proved for every solution, by evaluating at the times where `sin ωt = 1`.

**Proof status.** Proved. Not formalized: the energy transfer for a force acting during all time, beats near resonance, and the Lagrangian `½ m ẋ² − ½ k x² + x F(t)` with explicit dependence on time as an element of the jet ring. [Duhamel's formula](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Oscillations/Duhamel.lean) · [Primitives](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/TimePrimitive.lean)

## M49. Normal modes: the diagonalizing matrix is a hypothesis, its existence a separate theorem

**Observation.** §23 has two layers. The first is linear algebra over the reals: a positive definite form and a symmetric form are diagonalized together. The second uses nothing about how the matrix `S` was found, only `Sᵀ M S = 1` and `Sᵀ K S = diag λ`: the inverse of `S` is `Sᵀ M`, so the normal co-ordinates `Θ = Sᵀ M x` are defined without an inverse, `K S = M S diag λ` says that the columns are the modes, the characteristic equation follows from the determinant of `Sᵀ (K − c M) S`, and `Sᵀ (M ẍ + K x)` has the components `Θ̈ₐ + λₐ Θₐ`. Positivity of `K` enters only to make the `λₐ` positive, that is, to have real frequencies; the separation of the equations holds for every symmetric `K`, including unstable equilibria. Degenerate frequencies need no special treatment in this formulation, because nothing refers to eigenspaces.

**Interface decision.** Theorems of the second layer take `S`, `λ` and the two matrix identities as hypotheses, so they can be used with an explicitly known `S`; `exists_simultaneous_diagonalization` supplies them in general. The existence proof uses Mathlib's spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices over `ℝ` twice, through one lemma that restates it with transposes. The equations are stated for a family `x : n → TimeFunction` with matrices of constants `M.map (algebraMap ℝ TimeFunction)`, so that Mathlib's `mulVec` algebra applies. The hypothesis on `M` is Mathlib's `Matrix.PosDef`; `Mathlib.Algebra.Order.Star.Real` must be imported for its order lemmas on `ℝ`.

**Proof status.** Proved as stated. Proposition 70 and the derivation of `M ẍ + K x = 0` from the Lagrangian in the jet ring are in M61. Not formalized: the symmetry classification of §24. [Normal modes](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Oscillations/NormalModes.lean)

## M50. Parametric resonance: uniqueness comes from the Wronskian, existence is data

**Observation.** For `ẍ + p(t) x = 0` the Wronskian of two solutions has zero derivative by a computation in the ring, for any coefficient `p`. Given the two solutions with initial data `(1, 0)` and `(0, 1)`, the Wronskians of an arbitrary solution with them are constants, and these constants are its initial position and velocity; the identity `W(x, x₂) x₁ + W(x₁, x) x₂ = x W(x₁, x₂)` then gives the solution as a combination. So uniqueness for this linear equation with a variable coefficient needs no Grönwall estimate, once a fundamental system exists. Existence is the part that is not algebra, and it is taken as data. Liouville's theorem here is the constancy of one Wronskian. The stability statement splits into a statement about the equation, `Φ(s + nT) = Φ(s) Mⁿ`, and a statement about real `2 × 2` matrices of determinant one, whose powers are given by a Chebyshev recursion: `sin θ · Aᵏ⁺¹ = sin (k+1)θ · A − sin kθ · 1`. Boundedness for all `t ≥ 0`, not only at the times `nT`, uses that the fundamental system is bounded on one period.

**Interface decision.** `FundamentalSystem p` is a structure with the two solutions and their initial values; the oscillator supplies an instance, with trace `2 cos ωT`, so the structure is not empty and the criterion `|tr M| < 2` reads `sin ωT ≠ 0` there. Periodicity is `TimeFunction.shift T p = p`. The bound on the powers of a matrix is stated entrywise, to avoid a choice of matrix norm.

**Proof status.** Proved with a fundamental system as data. Not formalized: the existence of a fundamental system for a general smooth coefficient, unboundedness for `|tr M| > 2` and for `tr M = ±2` with `M ≠ ±1`, and the width of the instability zone, which is an asymptotic statement. [Parametric resonance](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Oscillations/Parametric.lean)

## M51. Regular precession is an identity between the body and the space frame

**Observation.** The regular precession of §33 is usually argued geometrically: `M`, `Ω` and the axis of the top are coplanar. In Eulerian angles it is a polynomial identity of the same kind as M42. The components of the fixed axis `Z` in the body frame are the third row of the rotation, `(sin θ sin ψ, sin θ cos ψ, cos θ)`, with no relation needed, and the rotation takes this vector to `(0, 0, 1)` using the relations for `θ` and `ψ` only; the angle `φ` does not enter, because a rotation about `Z` fixes `Z`. For a symmetric top the angular momentum in the body frame is `M` times this vector exactly when `θ̇ = 0`, `I₁ φ̇ = M` and `I₃ (φ̇ cos θ + ψ̇) = M cos θ`; the converse divides by `sin θ`, which is where the co-ordinates degenerate. What makes this the motion of a free top is the conservation of the angular momentum in the space frame, which is an equation of motion and not part of the kinematics.

**Interface decision.** The forward direction is stated over a commutative ring, with the two conditions on the rates as hypotheses `I₁ w₁ = M` and `I₃ (w₁ c₂ + w₃) = M c₂`, so that no division occurs. The equivalence is stated over the reals with `sin θ ≠ 0` and `I₁ ≠ 0`. The motion with uniform rates is given as an instance with real angles; its rates are those of the angle functions, and M42 identifies the vector of body angular velocity with the actual one.

**Proof status.** Proved; row 33 is *proved* with this reading. Not formalized: the derivation of `Ṁ = 0` for a free body from a Lagrangian on the rotation group (row 34), and the heavy symmetric top. [Regular precession](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Rigid/RegularPrecession.lean)

## M52. Stability of the extreme axes is a conserved quadratic form, not a linearization

**Observation.** For the free rigid body, the stability of the rotations about the axes of the greatest and the least moment needs no linearization and no estimate. The combination `2 Iₐ E − |L|²` of the two integrals is `Σᵢ (Iₐ/Iᵢ − 1) Lᵢ²`: the term of the axis `a` cancels, and for an extreme moment the two remaining coefficients have the same sign. So a definite quadratic form in the two transverse components is constant along every trajectory, and each transverse component is bounded for all time by the initial deviation. This is Lyapunov stability of the non-linear system, with the integral as the Lyapunov function. For the middle axis the form is indefinite and gives nothing; there the linearized equations have coefficients whose product is positive, which is the hyperbolic case. The instability of the non-linear motion about the middle axis is a further statement and is not proved.

**Interface decision.** The trajectory statements are for curves of the Lie–Poisson phase space with Euler's equations written out (`isTrajectory_kineticEnergy_iff`). The conserved sum is stated for a general axis `a`, and the bounds take the hypothesis that `Iₐ` is the greatest or the least moment as `∀ i, I i ≤ I a` or `∀ i, I a ≤ I i`. The linearized coefficients use `linearization` of M44 with the Lie–Poisson structure, computed directly from Euler's equations; they are stated at the axis `1`, the other axes being the same up to a cyclic renaming of the indices, which is not carried out.

**Proof status.** Proved as stated; row 37 stays *evidence* because the solution by elliptic functions is missing. Not formalized: the non-linear instability of the middle axis, and the linearized coefficients at the axes `0` and `2`. [Motions of the free rigid body](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Poisson/RigidBodyMotion.lean)

## M53. A substitution has a chain rule, and it was the missing lemma

**Observation.** A morphism out of a free C∞-ring on finitely many generators is a substitution `xₐ ↦ yₐ`. For a derivation `E` of the target, `E (s(y)) = Σₐ (∂ₐ s)(y) • E (yₐ)`. Earlier stages used only the special case in which the substitution intertwines two vector fields (`map_derivation_eq`), which is enough for conservation along trajectories. Three statements of this stage need the general case: the derivative of a function of the positions read in the jet ring or on the phase space, the time derivative of a function of `(q, α, t)` along a curve, and the derivative of `H(q, ∂S/∂q)` with respect to a parameter. The proof is the chain rule for one operation, applied to a presentation of the element.

**Interface decision.** `FreeSmoothRing.derivation_lift` is stated for a derivation with values in a module and `derivation_lift_mul` for a vector field. The index type is finite, so that the sum is over all generators; an element of a free ring on an infinite index type would need its support. The lemma is in a new file of the shared algebra layer, not in `FreeDerivation`, to avoid rebuilding the library.

**Proof status.** Proved. Not formalized: the same statement for a morphism into the dual numbers, which would give the tangent functor on morphisms (row 1). [The chain rule for a substitution](../ProjectAInos/SmoothAlgebra/FreeChainRule.lean)

## M54. The Legendre transformation goes from the phase space to the jet ring

**Observation.** As a map of spaces the Legendre transformation sends a velocity to a momentum. On rings of functions it goes the other way: it is the morphism from the phase space to the jet ring with `qᵢ ↦ qᵢ` and `pᵢ ↦ ∂L/∂q̇ᵢ`. For `L = Σ ½ mᵢ q̇ᵢ² − U(q)` it takes `Σ pᵢ²/2mᵢ + U` to the energy `Σ q̇ᵢ ∂L/∂q̇ᵢ − L`, and this uses `mᵢ ≠ 0` only to cancel `mᵢ²/mᵢ`. The correspondence of solutions is a statement about curves and does not use the morphism: a curve of the phase space is a trajectory exactly when its positions solve the Euler–Lagrange equations and its momenta are `mᵢ q̇ᵢ`. A potential has to be read in two rings, the jet ring and the phase space; the common source is the free ring on the positions, and the two readings are substitutions.

**Interface decision.** A potential is `u : FreeSmoothRing ι`, with `positionsToJet` and `positionsToPhase`. The masses are a family of non-zero reals, as in the existing `naturalLagrangian` and `PhaseSpace.kineticEnergy`; a kinetic form that depends on the position (M41) is not covered. The oscillator statements of M43 are instances with `u` quadratic, but they were proved before this stage and are not restated.

**Proof status.** Proved for natural Lagrangians with constant masses; row 40 stays *informal*. Not formalized: Theorem 51 for a hyperregular Lagrangian, the inverse morphism and the correspondence of the symplectic structures, and the Legendre transformation for a position-dependent kinetic form. ## M55. Jacobi's theorem without the implicit function theorem

**Observation.** Jacobi's theorem is usually stated with a step that solves `∂S/∂αᵢ = βᵢ` for `q(t)`. The mechanical content does not need that step. Given a curve `q(t)` along which the `∂S/∂αᵢ` are constant, differentiate the Hamilton–Jacobi equation with respect to `αᵢ` and compare with the time derivative of `∂S/∂αᵢ` along the curve: the difference is `Σₖ (∂²S/∂αᵢ∂qₖ)(q̇ₖ − ∂H/∂pₖ) = 0`, and non-degeneracy gives the first set of Hamilton's equations. The second set follows from the derivative with respect to `qᵢ`. Both derivatives are instances of M53, and the only other input is that mixed partial derivatives commute. Non-degeneracy enters as the statement that the matrix has no kernel over the ring of functions of time, which is what the argument uses; a non-vanishing determinant would imply it. No option that raises the limit is used.

**Proof status.** Proved; row 47 is *proved* with this reading. Not formalized: the construction of the curve from the equations `∂S/∂αᵢ = βᵢ`, a Hamiltonian that depends on time, and the separation of the variables of §48. ## M56. Liouville's theorem: the divergence is algebra, the measure is not

**Observation.** The divergence of a vector field on a free ring is `Σₐ ∂ₐ (X xₐ)`. For a Hamiltonian vector field of the canonical bracket it vanishes because mixed partial derivatives commute. With friction the divergence is `−Σᵢ ∂fᵢ/∂pᵢ`, a negative constant for the oscillator: the statement left open in M46. That the Lebesgue measure of a region is constant under the flow is a different statement, which needs the flow as a family of maps and a change of variables; for the oscillator the flow is a matrix of determinant one, which is the linear case.

**Interface decision.** `divergence` is defined for every vector field of a free ring on a finite index type, with respect to its generators; it is not invariant under a change of generators, and no claim of invariance is made.

**Proof status.** Proved in infinitesimal form; row 46 stays *informal*. Not formalized: the invariance of the measure under the flow of a Hamiltonian, and the two-form `ω` with `L_{X_H} ω = 0`. [Liouville's theorem](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Poisson/Liouville.lean)

## M57. Mechanical similarity is two morphisms and one exponent

**Observation.** The similarity of §10 uses two changes of scale: of the lengths, a morphism `xᵢ ↦ α xᵢ` of the ring of functions of the positions, and of the time, a morphism `f ↦ f(βt)` of the ring of functions of time. A potential is homogeneous of degree `k` when the first morphism multiplies it by `α^k`. Applying `∂ⱼ` to this identity and using the chain rule for a substitution (M53) gives that the gradient is homogeneous of degree `k − 1`; no derivative with respect to the scale `α` is taken. The derivative of `f(βt)` is `β f'(βt)`, so two time derivatives give `β²`, and Newton's equations are preserved exactly when `α β² = α^{k−1}`. Euler's relation `Σ qᵢ ∂U/∂qᵢ = kU`, which the virial identity uses, is the derivative of the homogeneity identity with respect to `α` at `α = 1`. That derivative is not available inside the ring, so the two notions of homogeneity are used side by side and not connected.

**Interface decision.** `IsHomogeneous k u` quantifies over `α > 0`, with a real exponent, so that degrees such as `−1` and non-integer degrees are covered; a homogeneous smooth function on all of the positions has a restricted set of possible degrees, and potentials with a singularity at the origin are outside this file (Kepler's problem is treated through its own equations, M59). The theorem is about solutions: `α γ(βt)` is a solution when `γ` is. The automorphism of the jet ring that scales `qᵢ⁽ⁿ⁾` by `α βⁿ` and preserves the Euler–Lagrange ideal is the corresponding algebraic statement and is not constructed.

**Proof status.** Proved on solutions; row 10 is *proved* with this reading. Not formalized: the scaling as an automorphism of the jet ring, the equivalence of the two notions of homogeneity, and the consequences for the times of motion in particular fields listed in §10. [Mechanical similarity](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Similarity.lean)

## M58. The virial theorem is the fundamental theorem of calculus divided by the time

**Observation.** The time average of the derivative of a bounded function tends to zero, because the integral of the derivative over `[0, τ]` is the increment, bounded by twice the bound, and it is divided by `τ`. With `G = Σ qᵢ pᵢ` and `Ġ = 2T − kU` along a trajectory, this is the virial theorem. The statement proved is about the average of the combination `2T − kU`. It does not assert that the averages of `T` and of `U` exist separately; when they do, the statement gives `2⟨T⟩ = k⟨U⟩`. The hypothesis is the boundedness of `Σ qᵢ pᵢ` along the trajectory, which holds when the positions and the momenta are bounded.

**Interface decision.** The limit is Mathlib's `Tendsto` along `atTop`, and the average is `(1/τ) ∫₀^τ` with the interval integral, as in M48. The general lemma about bounded functions of time is separate from the mechanical statement.

**Proof status.** Proved. [The virial theorem](../ProjectAInos/Mechanics/Poisson/VirialAverage.lean)

## M59. Kepler's third law: the similarity form is algebra, the period of an orbit is not

**Observation.** The equations of a Kepler motion with the inverse distance as a function of time (M37) are preserved by `q ↦ λ q(βt)`, `p ↦ λ β p(βt)`, `ρ ↦ ρ(βt)/λ` exactly when `β² λ³ = 1`. This is the third law in the form of §10: similar orbits have periods whose squares are as the cubes of their sizes. Stating the condition as `β² λ³ = 1` avoids real powers. The stronger statement of §15, that the period of an elliptic orbit is `2π a^{3/2} √(m/α)` whatever its eccentricity, compares orbits that are not similar; it needs the area of the ellipse and the areal velocity, and is not proved. The equation of the orbit, by contrast, costs nothing: the identity `A · q = |M|² − m α r` holds in every commutative ring, and along a motion `A` and `M` are constants.

**Interface decision.** `IsKeplerMotion.conic` is stated at each time with the values of `A` and `M` at time zero. `IsKeplerMotion.similar` takes `λ ≠ 0` and `β` with `β² λ³ = 1` as hypotheses; negative `λ` and `β` are allowed, since the equations permit them.

**Proof status.** Proved as stated; row 15 stays *evidence*. Not formalized: the period of an elliptic orbit, the classification of the conic by the sign of the energy, and the eccentricity as a function of `H` and `|M|²` along a motion. ## M60. A state is a morphism to the dual numbers, and constraints cut out their own tangent vectors

**Observation.** Landau–Lifshitz §1 says that a state is given by the co-ordinates and the velocities. In the language of C∞-rings a point is a morphism `A → ℝ` and a state is a morphism `A → ℝ[ε]`. For the free ring this is literally a pair `(a, v)`: the morphism `xᵢ ↦ aᵢ + ε vᵢ` takes `s` to `s(a) + ε Σ (∂ᵢ s)(a) vᵢ`, so a change of co-ordinates acts on the velocities by the Jacobian without that rule being postulated. For a ring presented by equations `eₖ = 0`, the universal property of the quotient gives the states at once: the pairs with `eₖ(a) = 0` and `Σ (∂ᵢ eₖ)(a) vᵢ = 0`. **Proof status.** Proved; row 1 is *proved* with the reading that a state is a morphism to `ℝ[ε]`. ## M61. Quadratic Hamiltonians: the degree is Euler's identity, and `sp(2n, ℝ)` is the map `S ↦ J S`

**Observation.** Proposition 70 has three statements of different kinds. (a) The degree. For elements of a C∞-ring the usable form of "homogeneous of degree `k`" is Euler's identity `E f = k f` with `E = Σ xₐ ∂ₐ`. The commutator `[∂ₐ, E] = ∂ₐ` says that a partial derivative lowers the degree by one, and since the matrix of the canonical bracket is constant, a bracket of elements of degrees `j` and `k` has the degree `j + k − 2`. No ring of polynomials is needed, and real degrees are covered. (b) The Lie algebra. A quadratic form is `Q_S = ½ xᵀ S x` with `S` symmetric, `{Q_S, Q_T} = Q_{SJT − TJS}`, and the Hamiltonian vector field of `Q_S` is the linear field `J S`. Because `J² = −1`, the map `S ↦ J S` is a bijection from the symmetric matrices to the matrices with `Aᵀ J + J A = 0`, and `J (SJT − TJS) = [JS, JT]`. So the isomorphism with `sp(2n, ℝ)` is matrix algebra once the bracket of two quadratic forms is computed. Mathlib's `sp` is defined with the matrix `−J`, which gives the same Lie algebra. (c) The normal form. From `Sᵀ M S = 1` and `Sᵀ K S = diag λ` one gets `M⁻¹ = S Sᵀ` and `K = (Sᵀ M)ᵀ (diag λ) (Sᵀ M)`, so `½ pᵀ M⁻¹ p + ½ qᵀ K q = Σ ½ (Pₐ² + λₐ Qₐ²)` with `P = Sᵀ p` and `Q = Sᵀ M q`. The transformation `q ↦ A q`, `p ↦ B p` satisfies `{Qₐ, P_b} = (A Bᵀ)ₐ_b`, so it is canonical exactly when `B` is the inverse transpose of `A`; here `A Bᵀ = Sᵀ M S = 1`. The equations `M ẍ + K x = 0` are reached from three sides that agree: the Euler–Lagrange expressions of `½ Σ (mᵢₖ vᵢ vₖ − kᵢₖ qᵢ qₖ)`, Hamilton's equations `q̇ = M⁻¹ p`, `ṗ = −K q`, and the normal co-ordinates of M49.

**Interface decision.** `IsEulerHomogeneous` is a property of an element of a free ring, with closure lemmas for generators, constants, sums and products, so a homogeneous polynomial is covered by applying them. `quadraticForm S` is defined for every matrix; theorems assume `S.IsSymm` where it matters, and `quadraticForm_injective` says that the symmetric matrix is determined by the form. The correspondence with `sp(2n, ℝ)` is stated through `spHamiltonian A = Q_{−JA}` and three theorems (the bracket, the linear vector field, injectivity), with membership in Mathlib's `LieAlgebra.Symplectic.sp` as the hypothesis. It is not bundled as a `LieEquiv`: a commutative ring carries Mathlib's commutator as its `Bracket` instance, which is zero, so a Lie algebra built from the Poisson bracket needs a type synonym. `linearTransformation A B` is a morphism of phase space, and an isomorphism is a pair of mutually inverse morphisms, as in M60. `quadraticHamiltonian W K` takes the inverse `W` of the mass matrix as an argument and is by definition the quadratic form of the block matrix `(K, 0; 0, W)`, so the lemmas on quadratic forms apply to it. The Lagrangian depends only on the symmetric parts of `mᵢₖ` and `kᵢₖ`; the formulas for the momenta and the equations are stated for symmetric matrices.

**Proof status.** Proved; row 23 is *proved*. Not formalized: the bundled isomorphism of Lie algebras, the expansion of a general Lagrangian to the second order at an equilibrium in several co-ordinates (the Hamiltonian side is M44), and the formal neighbourhood `ℝ[[q, p]]` of the manuscript's remark on perturbation theory. ## M62. The free particle: one constancy lemma carries the analysis, and isotropy needs three matrices

**Observation.** Theorem 66 says that a function `L` of the velocities whose boost `Σ uᵢ ∂L/∂vᵢ` is a total derivative `D F_u` of a function of `(t, q)` is a polynomial of degree at most two. Applying `∂/∂vⱼ` gives `Σ uᵢ ∂²L/∂vᵢ∂vⱼ = ∂F_u/∂qⱼ`, because `[∂/∂vⱼ, D] = ∂/∂qⱼ` and `F_u` has order zero. The left side has no derivative with respect to `t` or `q`, the right side none with respect to `v`, so every partial derivative of this element vanishes. The manuscript then says "both are constant". In the free C∞-ring this is a lemma with analytic content: an element with vanishing partial derivatives is a constant. It is proved by restricting to the segment between two points, where the element becomes a function of time with zero derivative; the chain rule along the curve is the morphism lemma `map_derivation_eq`, and the constancy of a function of time with zero derivative was already available. The same lemma, applied to `∂L/∂vⱼ − Σ mⱼₖ vₖ` and then to `L − ½ Σ mᵢⱼ vᵢ vⱼ − Σ bᵢ vᵢ`, gives the polynomial; no Taylor expansion is used. For isotropy, three orthogonal matrices suffice: `−1` gives `b = 0`, the change of sign of one co-ordinate gives `mᵢⱼ = 0` for `i ≠ j`, and the exchange of two co-ordinates gives `mᵢᵢ = mⱼⱼ`. So the hypothesis used is invariance under the signed permutations, a finite subgroup of the orthogonal group.

**Interface decision.** The hypothesis `DependsOnVelocities L` replaces the first two steps of §4 (homogeneity of space and of time); it is stated with the partial derivatives of the jet ring, not with a morphism from a ring of functions of `v`. The conclusion is written with the existing `naturalLagrangian (fun _ => μ) 0`. ## M63. Galileo's principle: on a free ring the commutators are checked on generators, and one relation moves the Euler operators

**Observation.** The prolongation `pr ξ = Σₖ Σᵢ (Dᵏ ξᵢ) ∂ᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾` existed as a function with a finite-support sum. It is a vector field in every jet ring, with the chain rule proved as for the total derivative. On the standard jet ring a vector field is determined by its values on the generators, so the same field is the derivation with `t ↦ 0` and `qᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾ ↦ Dᵏ ξᵢ`, and two facts that are computations with binomial sums in an abstract jet ring become checks on generators: `[pr ξ, D] = 0`, and the commutator of two prolongations. For `ξₗ = Σ Aₗₘ qₘ + aₗ + t uₗ` the derivatives are `Dⁿ ξₗ = Σ Aₗₘ qₘ⁽ⁿ⁾ + Dⁿ(aₗ + t uₗ)`, and `Dⁿ(a + t u)` is `a + t u`, `u`, `0`. The commutators `[X_{A,a,u}, X_{A',a',u'}] = X_{A'A − AA', A'a − Aa', A'u − Au'}` and `[∂ₜ, X_{A,a,u}] = X_{0,u,0}` are the Lie algebra of the affine maps extended by the boosts and the time translation; the Galilei algebra is the part with `A` antisymmetric. Boosts and translations commute here. The relation `{Kᵢ, Pⱼ} = m δᵢⱼ` of the Poisson side (M30) is the central extension; it does not appear for vector fields, because a constant has zero Hamiltonian vector field. For the Euler operators one relation suffices: if `X` commutes with `D` and `[∂ₗ⁽ᵏ⁾, X] = Σᵢ cₗᵢ ∂ᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾` with constants `cₗᵢ` that do not depend on `k`, then `Eₗ(X F) = X(Eₗ F) + Σᵢ cₗᵢ Eᵢ(F)`. With `Eₗ(D F) = 0` this gives the content of the principle: a field that changes `L` by a total derivative maps the Euler–Lagrange ideal of `L` into itself. For the Galilei fields `cₗᵢ = Aᵢₗ`, and translations, boosts and `∂ₜ` have `c = 0`.

**Interface decision.** The fields are parametrized by a matrix and two vectors, `galileiField A a u`, without the antisymmetry of `A` in the type; the general linear case costs nothing and the commutator formula is uniform. Antisymmetry enters as a hypothesis where it is used: `mᵢ Aᵢⱼ + mⱼ Aⱼᵢ = 0` for the quasi-invariance of a natural Lagrangian, and `Aᵀ = −A` in `IsInertial`. `JetRing.euler_apply` and `JetRing.apply_mem_eulerLagrangeIdeal` are stated for an abstract jet ring and an arbitrary vector field with the two commutation hypotheses, so they also cover `∂ₜ`. `IsInertial L` is a property of a Lagrangian, the invariance of its Euler–Lagrange ideal; the statement that such a frame exists is the premise of §3 and is not formalized as a theorem. The lemma that a finite sum of derivations is evaluated termwise moved from the dissipation file to the shared `Derivation` file, where the module structure is defined.

## M64. Hyperregular means that the Legendre transformation has an inverse morphism, and the rest is the chain rule

**Observation.** Theorem 51 is marked as analysis in the manuscript, because for a given Lagrangian one needs the inverse function theorem, or convexity and properness, to know that the Legendre transformation `FL` is a diffeomorphism. Once that is granted, nothing analytic remains. In the language of rings, `FL` is a morphism from the functions of `(q, p)` to the functions of `(q, v)` with `qᵢ ↦ qᵢ` and `pᵢ ↦ ∂L/∂vᵢ`, and hyperregularity is the existence of an inverse morphism `G`. The Hamiltonian is `H = G(E)`. Applying `∂/∂vⱼ` to `FL(G(vₖ)) = vₖ` with the chain rule for a substitution (M53) gives `Σᵢ FL(∂(G vₖ)/∂pᵢ) ∂²L/∂vᵢ∂vⱼ = δₖⱼ`: the matrix `∂²L/∂v∂v` has an inverse over the ring. This is the regularity condition, obtained as a consequence. Applying `∂/∂vⱼ` to `FL(H) = E` gives `Σᵢ (FL(∂H/∂pᵢ) − vᵢ) ∂²L/∂vᵢ∂vⱼ = 0`, so `FL(∂H/∂pᵢ) = vᵢ` by that invertibility, and `∂/∂qⱼ` then gives `FL(∂H/∂qⱼ) = −∂L/∂qⱼ`. For curves the two directions use the two morphisms: along a curve with `pᵢ = ∂L/∂vᵢ(γ, γ̇)`, evaluating on the curve `(γ, γ̇)` after `FL` is evaluating on the curve of the phase space; along a curve with `q̇ᵢ = ∂H/∂pᵢ`, evaluating on the phase curve after `G` is evaluating on `(γ, γ̇)`, because `G(vᵢ) = ∂H/∂pᵢ`. The symplectic form `ω_L` is the canonical bracket transported by `FL`, and its only non-trivial entry is `{qᵢ, vⱼ} = (∂²L/∂v∂v)⁻¹ᵢⱼ`.

## M65. A generating function gives two morphisms, and canonicity is the symmetry of two Hessians

**Observation.** A generating function `F(q, Q)` is an element of the ring of functions of the old and the new positions. It gives two morphisms from a phase space to that ring: `α` with `qᵢ ↦ qᵢ`, `pᵢ ↦ ∂F/∂qᵢ`, and `β` with `Qᵢ ↦ Qᵢ`, `Pᵢ ↦ −∂F/∂Qᵢ`. The transformation is free when `α` has an inverse morphism `G`, so that `(q, Q)` are co-ordinates on the phase space, and it is then the morphism `G ∘ β`, which writes a function of `(Q, P)` in the variables `(q, p)`. As for the Legendre transformation (M64), the chain rule applied to `α(G(Qⱼ)) = Qⱼ` shows that the mixed matrix `M = ∂²F/∂q∂Q` has an inverse `B` over the ring; the non-degeneracy condition of the textbooks is a consequence here. The chain rule also gives the derivatives of `G x` for every function `x` of `(q, Q)`: `∂(Gx)/∂p = (∂x/∂Q) B` and `∂(Gx)/∂q = ∂x/∂q − (∂(Gx)/∂p) S` with `S = ∂²F/∂q∂q`. Substituting in the canonical bracket, the terms with `S` cancel because `S` is symmetric, and the bracket in the co-ordinates `(q, Q)` is `{x, y} = ∂x/∂q · (∂y/∂Q · B) − (∂x/∂Q · B) · ∂y/∂q`. With this formula `{Qᵢ, Qⱼ} = 0` is immediate, `{Qᵢ, Pⱼ} = δᵢⱼ` is `B M = 1`, and `{Pᵢ, Pⱼ} = 0` is the symmetry of `∂²F/∂Q∂Q`. So the proof uses that mixed partial derivatives commute, three times, and nothing else; the differential form `p dq − P dQ = dF` does not appear. Only the inverse of `α` is needed for canonicity. The inverse of `β` is needed only to say that the transformation is invertible.

**Interface decision.** `PositionPair ι` is the free ring on `ι ⊕ ι`, the same type as the phase space, with its own names for the generators. `IsFree F G` carries the inverse as an argument, as `IsHyperregular` does. Vectors and matrices over the ring are Mathlib's, so the symmetry argument is `dotProduct_mulVec` and `vecMul_transpose`. The final statement is `IsPoissonHom` for the canonical structure, proved on generators. `exchange = Σ qᵢ Qᵢ` is the non-degenerate example: for it `α` is the identity morphism, and the transformation is `Qᵢ = pᵢ`, `Pᵢ = −qᵢ`. The identity transformation has no generating function of this kind, which is why textbooks introduce the other kinds; they are not treated.

**Proof status.** Proved; row 45 is *proved* for free generating functions that do not depend on time. Not formalized: generating functions of the kinds `F(q, P)`, `F(p, Q)`, `F(p, P)`, time-dependent generating functions with `H' = H + ∂F/∂t`, the converse that an invertible mixed matrix gives a free generating function locally, and the canonical property of the flow at a finite time. ## M66. Separation of the variables is one identity, and the co-ordinate systems are outside the free ring

**Observation.** The verification that a separated action solves the Hamilton–Jacobi equation is the same computation in spherical, parabolic and elliptic co-ordinates. For `H = Σᵢ Ψᵢ (gᵢ pᵢ²/2m + uᵢ)` with `Σᵢ Ψᵢ Φᵢⱼ = δⱼ,ⱼ₀`, if `gᵢ (∂S/∂qᵢ)²/2m + uᵢ = Σⱼ Φᵢⱼ αⱼ` then `H(q, ∂S/∂q) = Σⱼ (Σᵢ Ψᵢ Φᵢⱼ) αⱼ = αⱼ₀`. Only one row of the inverse of `Φ` enters, and nothing in the verification uses that `Φᵢⱼ` depends on `qᵢ` alone; that condition is what makes the separated equations ordinary differential equations. The spherical case is the nested form `p₀²/2m + a + ρ (p₁²/2m + b + σ (p₂²/2m + c))` with the unipotent matrix `Φ = (1, −ρ, 0; 0, 1, −σ; 0, 0, 1)`, and it separates for arbitrary functions `ρ` and `σ`. The parabolic and the elliptic cases are the form of Liouville with a cyclic third co-ordinate, which needs `λ (w₀ + w₁) = 1`. The classical coefficients `1/r²`, `1/sin²θ`, `1/(ξ + η)` are not elements of the free C∞-ring on three generators: they are smooth on open subsets only. So the classical systems are covered in form and not as instances. A second observation is about elaboration. A morphism with an implicit index type, such as `positionsToAction : FreeSmoothRing ι → ActionSpace ι`, is slow to apply: the coercion to a function is searched while `ι` is still a metavariable, and each application took more than a second, enough to exhaust the default heartbeats in a statement with a dozen applications. With the index type as an explicit argument the same file compiles more than three times faster (from 32 to 9 seconds, with more statements in it). `toJet`, `positionsToJet` and `positionsToPhase` have the same shape and the same cost.

**Interface decision.** The coefficient functions are elements of the free ring on the co-ordinates, read on the phase space by `positionsToPhase` and in the functions of `(q, α, t)` by `positionsToAction ι`. The general theorem takes the matrix `Φ` and the index `j₀` of the energy as data; the two cases on `Fin 3` fix them and restate the hypotheses as three separated equations with `kinetic m S i` for `(∂S/∂qᵢ)²/2m`. The action is a hypothesis: the theorem says that an action with these derivatives is a solution, and it does not construct one. Row 48 is closed with this remainder and keeps its status, because the main target names the co-ordinate systems.

**Proof status.** Proved as stated. Not formalized: the Hamiltonians of the three co-ordinate systems as elements of rings of functions on open subsets, the quadratures, the completeness of the integrals, and the motion obtained from them by Jacobi's theorem (M55). ## M67. Molecules and the rigid body: what the linear algebra gives, and what needs a group or a configuration space

**Observation.** In §24 two statements are linear algebra. A symmetry of the system is a matrix that commutes with `M` and `K`; it maps solutions of `M ẍ + K x = 0` to solutions and preserves the space `{v : K v = c M v}` of the modes of each frequency, so these spaces are representations of the symmetry group and their dimensions are constrained by it. And the number of non-zero frequencies is the rank of `K`, because `Sᵀ K S = diag λ` with `S` invertible; the count `3N − 6` of the vibrations of a non-linear molecule is this statement together with the fact that the kernel of `K` is the six-dimensional space of the translations and the rotations. The rest of §24 is the character theory of point groups. In §34 the equations `Ṁ = K` in the body frame are the Lie–Poisson equations, already proved for an arbitrary Hamiltonian `H(L, Γ)`: the torque is `Γ × ∂H/∂Γ`. The equation `Ṗ = F` and forces depending on the position need the position of the centre of mass and the rotation as variables, that is, the configuration space `ℝ³ ⋊ SO(3)`, which the plan excludes (decision D3).

**Interface decision.** A symmetry is a single matrix with two commutation hypotheses; no group and no representation type is introduced, so the statements apply to every element of a symmetry group. `modeSpace M K c` is a `Submodule`, so that Mathlib's representation theory can be applied to it later. The count of the non-zero frequencies uses Mathlib's `Matrix.rank`. No new declaration was needed for §34; its row now states which existing theorems are the equations of motion and why the remainder is outside the model.

**Proof status.** Rows 24 and 34 are closed with a stated remainder and keep their status. Not formalized: characters and the multiplicities of the irreducible representations in the space of the modes, selection of the modes by symmetry type, the kernel of `K` for a molecule, `Ṗ = F`, and the Lagrangian on `T(SO(3))`. ## M68. The asymptotic sections: one exact identity, and statements without an error estimate

**Observation.** Sections 20, 29, 30, 49 and 51 state results of approximation: a first-order formula for the angle of deflection, an amplitude equation from successive approximations, an effective potential from averaging, the conservation of the adiabatic invariant, and the exponential smallness of its change. In each the text gives the leading term and no bound for the error, so there is no theorem to formalize until a statement with an estimate is chosen; this is what tier P means in the plan. One exact statement is available in §49. For `ẍ + ω(t)² x = 0` the action variable `I = (ẋ² + ω² x²)/2ω` satisfies `İ = (ω̇/2ω²)(ω² x² − ẋ²)` along every solution. The identity shows the two facts on which the adiabatic argument rests: `İ` has the factor `ω̇`, and the other factor has zero mean over a period of the unperturbed motion. The argument itself, that the integral of this product over a time of order `1/ε` is of order `ε`, is the averaging estimate. Sections 27, 28, 37 and 52 have proved parts and remainders of other kinds: an existence theorem for linear differential equations, formal power series as a quotient by flat functions, elliptic functions, and the theorem of Liouville and Arnold.

**Interface decision.** The inverse of the frequency is a second function of time with the hypothesis `ω ωinv = 1`, as for the inverse distance in Kepler's problem (M37); no division of functions of time is defined. The equation is Hill's equation `IsHill (ω * ω) x` of the parametric resonance file, so the same solutions are meant. In the ledger the rows of tier P and D state the result that would be the theorem and the reason why it is not proved, and they are listed as closed with a stated remainder.

**Proof status.** The identity is proved. Rows 20, 27, 28, 29, 30, 37, 49, 51 and 52 keep their status. Not formalized: every estimate of an error in these sections, the action variable of a general system as an integral over a closed curve, and the theorems named in the rows.
