# General Relativity Formalization Notes

These notes record mathematical distinctions and interface decisions in `ProjectAinos/GeneralRelativity` and in the Lorentz geometry that it uses. They complement the [research plan](cinfty-general-relativity.md) with its [ledger](cinfty-general-relativity.md#ledger), the [guide](general-relativity.md) and the [smooth-geometry notes](smooth-geometry-notes.md). Each entry distinguishes proved results from targets. Add an observation that changes an interface, or corrects an earlier judgement, as a numbered entry with a link to the proof.

## GR01. In two dimensions the differential Bianchi identity and the Einstein tensor are empty

**Observation.** The only curved metric with computed curvature is the conformal plane `exp(2x²)(−dt² + dx²)`, of dimension two. The cyclic sum `(∇_X R)(Y, Z) + (∇_Y R)(Z, X) + (∇_Z R)(X, Y)` is antisymmetric in its three vectors, because `(∇_X R)(Y, Z)` is antisymmetric in `Y` and `Z`; in dimension two it is therefore zero for every tensor with this antisymmetry, whatever the connection. In dimension two the Ricci tensor is also `(R/2) g`, so the Einstein tensor is zero: for the conformal plane `ricci_component` gives `diag(2, −2)` and `scalarCurvature_eq` gives `−4 exp(−2x²)`. A regression on this example cannot detect a wrong sign or a missing term in either statement.

**Interface decision.** The differential Bianchi identity, the Einstein tensor and the variation of the Einstein–Hilbert action are tested on a curved metric of dimension four, built from coefficients on a co-ordinate domain. The plan asked for "nonzero-curvature regressions"; the dimension is the additional requirement.

**Proof status.** The four-dimensional example is proved (F10): `ExponentialConformalExample.metric` is `e^{2z}(−dt² + dx² + dy² + dz²)` on `ℝ⁴`, with `Ric = diag(2, −2, −2, 0)` (`metric_ricci_chartField`), `R = −6 e^{−2z}` (`metric_scalarCurvature`), `G = diag(−1, 1, 1, 3)` (`metric_einstein_chartField`, so `metric_einstein_ne_zero`), `∇_{∂_z} R = −2 R` (`metric_curvatureDerivative_dz`, so `metric_curvatureDerivative_ne_zero`) and, through the contracted identity, `∇^a Ric_{az} = 6 e^{−2z}` (`metric_ricci_divergence`). The variation of the Einstein–Hilbert action is tested on it (F11): for `h = f η`, `ΔS'(0) = −(1/2κ) ∫ f (6 + 4Λ e^{2z})`, where `6 = η^{ik} G_{ik}` (`EinsteinHilbert.hasDerivAt_relativeAction_conformal`), so the derivative is not zero for a bump function even without a cosmological term. [Conformal plane](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/ConformalExample.lean) · [Curvature](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/Curvature.lean) · [Four-dimensional example](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/ExponentialConformalExample.lean)

## GR02. There is no integral of a density on a manifold

**Observation.** The library has the volume density of a metric on the tangent spaces (`volumeDensity`), its expression in a chart (`chartDensity`), the formula of the divergence with it (`divergence_density`), and Stokes' formula for a form with compact support on a co-ordinate space (`integral_exteriorDerivative_basis_zero`). It has integrals along curves. It has no integral of a function or a density over a manifold, and neither has the pinned Mathlib. The action of the gravitational field and the action of the Maxwell field on a curved background are such integrals.

**Interface decision.** The identities of the variation (of the inverse metric, of the density, of the connection, of the scalar curvature) are proved at each point of a manifold. The integrated statement is made first for a metric on the model space and a variation with compact support, where the integral is the Lebesgue integral of the co-ordinates and the existing Stokes formula applies as it stands; then for an open set of the model space; then for a variation supported in a chart of a manifold. The ledger row records how far the statement reaches. An integral that does not depend on the chart, with a partition of unity, is a separate construction.

**Proof status.** The integrated statement is proved for a metric given by coefficients on an open set of the model space (F11), which covers the first two steps; the identities are proved in the co-ordinates of that set, not at each point of a manifold (GR10). The variation supported in a chart of a manifold is a target. ## GR03. A function of the positions on the phase space needs its index type

**Observation.** `PhaseSpace.ofPosition` was a `SmoothRingHom (FreeSmoothRing ι) (PhaseSpace ι)` with `ι` implicit. Lean elaborates the leaves of `ofPosition (g μ ν) * p ν` before it unifies the product, so every occurrence searched the coercion to a function with a metavariable in the type: about 1.7 s each, and a `have` with a sum of such terms in the geodesic Hamiltonian exceeded the heartbeat limit. This is the case of the contributor rule on index types that no other argument determines.

**Interface decision.** `ofPosition ι` takes the index type explicitly. The uses in the electrodynamics layer and its checks now name it, and the statements have not changed otherwise. `lift_ofPosition` now evaluates a function of the positions along any substitution, not only at a point, in place of a local lemma of the Hamiltonian of a charge, and `ofPosition_injective` is stated beside it.

**Proof status.** Proved and built. [Position](../ProjectAinos/SmoothAlgebra/Poisson/Position.lean) · [Hamiltonian of a charge](../ProjectAinos/Electrodynamics/Hamiltonian.lean)

## GR04. The geodesic Hamiltonian is stated with both matrices, and its trajectories are complete

**Observation.** In the canonical phase space the Hamiltonian `½ g^{μν} p_μ p_ν` needs the inverse metric, and the geodesic equation needs the metric. A free C∞-ring has no inverse of a matrix, and an identity in a free ring can be unsatisfiable while the statement type-checks. The bracket of a momentum with `H` is `−½ (L_ξ g⁻¹)^{μν} p_μ p_ν`: the Lie derivative of the inverse metric appears, and `L_ξ g⁻¹ = −g⁻¹ (L_ξ g) g⁻¹` turns it into the Killing equation of the metric. The relation `ṗ_l = ½ ∂_l g_{αβ} ẋ^α ẋ^β` follows by differentiating `p_μ = g_{μν} ẋ^ν` in `x^l`, without the derivative of the inverse matrix. A trajectory is a curve of `TimeFunction`, smooth for all values of the parameter: in the chart `r = 2M + eˢ` of the Schwarzschild plane every radial geodesic along which `r` changes, and every ray that enters `r < 3M`, reaches `r = 2M` at a finite parameter and is not a trajectory.

**Interface decision.** The theorems with the metric take the coefficients `gm` and `gi` with the hypotheses that `gm` is symmetric and `Σ_λ gm μ λ * gi λ ν = δ`; the symmetry of `gi` and the other inverse relation are proved from them (`inverse_symm`, `inverse_mul`). The theorems that need only `H` (`canonical_q_hamiltonian`, `isIntegral_vectorMomentum_iff`, `lieInverse_symm`) take the symmetry of `gi` and no `gm`. The Killing condition is the Killing equation of the chart, `lieMetric ξ gm = 0`; `of_lieInverse` and `of_lieMetric` relate it to `lieInverse ξ gi = 0`, the form that the bracket gives. `isTrajectory_iff` is the comparison with the geodesic equation of the chart. The proved instances are `isTrajectory_rest`, the curve at rest with zero momenta for every metric, `isTrajectory_line`, the straight lines of constant coefficients, and the equatorial plane of the exterior Schwarzschild field with diagonal matrices smooth on all of `ℝ³`. The red shift along a geodesic segment, the case of a radial ray, is the native statement of GR06.

**Proof status.** Proved for charts whose domain is all of `ℝⁿ` and for trajectories defined for all values of the parameter. The nonconstant trajectories of the Schwarzschild plane (the photon circle `r = 3M`, the rays scattered from infinity) exist but are not constructed. The comparison of `IsCoordinateGeodesic` with the native geodesics of a Lorentz manifold is not proved; it needs the coordinate metric of a chart whose coefficients come from the free ring, and it is deferred to F14, where the orbits of the Schwarzschild field use both layers. [GeodesicHamiltonian](../ProjectAinos/GeneralRelativity/GeodesicHamiltonian.lean) · [SchwarzschildEquatorial](../ProjectAinos/GeneralRelativity/SchwarzschildEquatorial.lean)

## GR05. An observer measures with the spatial form, whose square root is the radar distance

**Observation.** The distance that an observer with the velocity `u` measures is the metric of the part of a displacement orthogonal to `u`, `g(v, w) − g(u, v) g(u, w)/g(u, u)`. Landau–Lifshitz derive it from a light signal sent to a neighbouring event and back: the two null displacements `v − τ₁ u` and `v − τ₂ u` are the roots of a quadratic in `τ`, half the proper time between them is `√(γ_u(v, v))`, and the middle event is simultaneous with `v`. Its positivity is the statement that a nonzero vector orthogonal to a timelike vector is spacelike, which holds in Minkowski space by the inequality of Cauchy–Schwarz and transfers to every event by a Lorentz frame. The observer at rest in a chart has the velocity `e₀`, which is timelike only where `g₀₀ < 0`; in rotating co-ordinates this is the inside of the light cylinder `Ω²ρ² < 1`.

**Interface decision.** The spatial form is a bilinear form on the model space (`Minkowski.spatialForm`), applied to `g.form x` at an event, and the radar statements are in the same tangent space. Its positivity takes a Lorentz frame of the convention `.timeNegative`, so it holds for every `LorentzMetric .timeNegative` and every `CoordinateMetric .timeNegative`. The positivity of the spatial metric of the observer at rest takes `g₀₀ < 0` as a hypothesis; the clock formula `properTime_atRest` is an identity and needs a curve at rest only on `[a, b]`. The rotating metric is defined only on the domain inside the light cylinder. Lengths of finite paths are stated where the spatial metric does not depend on `x⁰`, for the circle about the rotation axis.

**Proof status.** Proved. [SpatialForm](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/SpatialForm.lean) · [ObserverMeasurement](../ProjectAinos/GeneralRelativity/ObserverMeasurement.lean) · [RotatingFrame](../ProjectAinos/GeneralRelativity/RotatingFrame.lean)

## GR06. Killing fields are native, and the red shift is stated along a geodesic segment

**Observation.** The plan put §88 on a native Killing layer: the Killing equation on a Lorentz manifold and the conservation of `g(K, γ̇)` along an affine geodesic, with the frequencies of stationary observers along a supplied ray. The library already has the derivative of a pairing along a curve (`hasDerivAt_pairing_along`), the covariant derivative of an ambient field along a curve (`covariantAlong_ambient`) and geodesics on any set of parameters (`IsGeodesicOn`); with these the conservation is the product rule. The co-ordinate field `∂_k` of a chart satisfies the Killing equation where `∂_k g_{ij} = 0`, by metric compatibility and the vanishing torsion, since the brackets of co-ordinate fields vanish.

**Interface decision.** `LorentzMetric.IsKillingField` is pointwise and asks no regularity; the conservation theorems take the differentiability of `K`. The red shift is stated with the unit velocity `u = K/N` of the stationary observer and `ω = −g(u, γ̇)`, for the convention `.timeNegative`. For a coordinate metric the criterion is the invariance of the form under the translations along `x^k` (`CoordinateMetric.isKillingField_of_invariant`). The planned `Redshift.lean` is part of `Killing.lean`. The instances are the exterior Schwarzschild field on `r > 2M`, `0 < θ < π` (`SchwarzschildExterior`), where the red shift holds along every geodesic segment, radial rays included, and the rotating frame, where `∂_t` is a Killing field and the observers at rest see the frequency shift of the disc.

**Proof status.** Proved. The existence of a geodesic between two given events is not part of the statements; the geodesic is given. [Killing](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/Killing.lean) · [SchwarzschildExterior](../ProjectAinos/GeneralRelativity/SchwarzschildExterior.lean)

## GR07. The Newtonian family uses the shared frame criterion and chart

**Observation.** `NewtonianLorentz.lean` had private copies of the signature criterion for `d = 3` and of the lemmas of the chart of an open set. The shared `Minkowski.exists_lorentzFrame` and `CoordinateMetric.physicalChart` prove the same statements in every dimension.

**Interface decision.** The private copies are removed. `NewtonianMetricFamily.physicalChart` is now the shared chart, and its lemmas keep their names and statements and are proved by the shared ones. The metric of the family is still built by hand: making it `CoordinateMetric.toLorentzMetric` of a coordinate metric would also replace its component, inverse and Christoffel lemmas by the shared ones, and is left as a follow-up.

**Proof status.** Proved and built. [NewtonianLorentz](../ProjectAinos/GeneralRelativity/NewtonianLorentz.lean) · [CoordinateMetric](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/CoordinateMetric.lean)

## GR08. The covariant derivative of the curvature is taken from the smooth tensor, with the direction last

**Observation.** The curvature is defined from `curvatureField`, the commutator of the connection on locally `C²` fields. Differentiating that expression as a field would need `C³` arguments and a finite-order smoothness lemma for `curvatureField`; the library has only the `C^∞` lemma `contMDiffAt_curvatureField`. The Leibniz correction `∇_u(R(X, Y)Z) − R(∇_u X, Y)Z − R(X, ∇_u Y)Z − R(X, Y)∇_u Z` of the smooth tensor `g.curvature` needs only `C¹` arguments, and it is linear over scalar germs in each of the three slots. The second Bianchi identity does not need an inertial chart: the co-ordinate fields of any chart commute (`chartField_bracket`), so on them `R(B, C) = [∇_B, ∇_C]`, and the cyclic sum of the triple covariant derivatives cancels in pairs.

**Interface decision.** `Tensor.ContMDiffTensorialAt.mkHom₃` builds a continuous trilinear map from an operation that is tensorial in three slots, and `Tensor.trilinearCovariantDerivative connection R hR` is the Leibniz correction for any connection on the tangent bundle and any field `R` of tangent-valued trilinear maps that is a smooth section of `trilinearBundle` (`IsSmoothTrilinear R`). The direction comes last, as in `bilinearCovariantDerivative` and `twoFormDerivative`: `g.curvatureDerivative x v w z u = (∇_u R)(v, w)z`. Landau–Lifshitz write the transported vector first and the antisymmetric pair last, so this is their `R^i_{klm;n}` with `k` the slot of `z`, `(l, m)` the pair `(v, w)` and `n` the direction; the library's `R^l_{ijk} = (R(∂_i, ∂_j)∂_k)^l` is their `R^l_{kij}`, and `Ric_{jk} = R^i_{ijk}` in the library's order is their `R_{kj} = R^i_{kij}`. `curvature_second_bianchi` is stated for arbitrary tangent vectors and reduced to co-ordinate fields of `chartAt`; no Jacobi identity, inertial chart or second partial derivative is used.

Only these types are constructed: the pinned Mathlib induces no connection on the dual, hom or tensor bundles of a bundle with a connection, so the derivatives of covectors, of contravariant and mixed tensors and of the general shapes of `Tensor.Shape` remain, and the ledger row of §85 is closed with this remainder.

**Proof status.** Proved and built. [Three slots](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Tensor/TrilinearTensoriality.lean) · [Trilinear derivative](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Tensor/TrilinearDerivative.lean) · [Curvature derivative](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/CurvatureDerivative.lean)

## GR09. The contracted identity is proved at an inertial centre, with an inverse metric that carries no proof

**Observation.** The library writes the components of the inverse metric as `inverseComponent x (chartTangentEquiv he hy)`, which depends on the proof `hy` that the point lies in the chart; it cannot be differentiated as a function of the point. At the centre of an inertial chart the Christoffel symbols vanish, so the covariant derivatives of tensor components are their partial derivatives, but the contracted identity also differentiates `g^{ij}`. The Ricci tensor exists as a `CovariantTwoTensor`, whose bundle is spelled differently from `Tensor.SmoothBilinear`, the type that `bilinearCovariantDerivative` differentiates.

**Interface decision.** `chartInverse g e x i j = dx^i(♯dx^j)` is a function of the point; `contract_chart` identifies the contraction with it on the chart source, and `IsInertialChartAt.mvfderiv_chartInverse` gives `d(g^{ij}) = 0` at an inertial centre, because the raised co-ordinate covectors are parallel there. The second Bianchi identity contracted twice is then an identity between real arrays, a private lemma of `Einstein.lean`. `ricciBilinear` and `einsteinBilinear` are built from `ricci_smooth` and `einstein_smooth` as `Tensor.SmoothBilinear`, not by a coercion from `ricciTensor`. `bilinearDivergenceCovector g B x b = ∇^a B_{ab}` contracts the first slot with the direction; the statements hold in either convention and every dimension, and `ricci_eq_zero_of_einstein_eq_zero` records that the trace `(1 − n/2) R` of the Einstein tensor is lost only in dimension two.

**Proof status.** Proved and built. [Inertial derivatives](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/InertialDerivatives.lean) · [Einstein tensor](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/Einstein.lean)

## GR10. The metric is varied through its coefficients, and each varied metric is read natively

**Observation.** GR02 planned the identities of the variation at each point of a manifold, and the research plan derived `δΓ` without co-ordinates, from the uniqueness of the Levi-Civita connection. Both need a family of native Lorentz metrics `g_ε` whose curvature is differentiated in `ε`. The library has no such family: `LorentzMetric` is a single metric, the curvature of `g_ε` lives on the tangent spaces of the manifold with `ε` outside, and a derivative in `ε` of `scalarCurvature` would have to be built first. For a metric given by coefficients on an open set `U` of the model space, every quantity of `g + εh` is a real function of `(ε, x)`: the coefficients are jointly smooth, so the Christoffel symbols, the Ricci tensor, the scalar curvature and the density are jointly smooth where `det ≠ 0`, and their derivatives in `ε` exchange with the derivatives in `x` (`ContDiffAt.isSymmSndFDerivAt`).

**Interface decision.** The variation is computed with the textbook formulas in the coefficients (`christoffelOf`, `ricciOf`, `scalarOf`, `densityOf`, and `familyChristoffel` and its relatives on the slices of a family), and each formula is identified with the native quantity of the coordinate metric (`toLorentzMetric_chartChristoffel`, `toLorentzMetric_ricci_chartField`, `toLorentzMetric_scalarCurvature`, `toLorentzMetric_chartDensity`, `toLorentzMetric_einstein_chartField`). For every admissible `ε`, `scalarDensity_eq_toLorentzMetric` reads the integrand as the native density of `g.addVariation h hh ε hε`. The Palatini identity is an identity between arrays of numbers (`palatini_algebra`), with metric compatibility in the form `∂_e g^{bc} = −Γ^b_{ed} g^{dc} − Γ^c_{ed} g^{bd}`, so the divergence vector is `V^a = g^{bc} δΓ^a_{bc} − g^{ab} δΓ^c_{cb}`, written with `δΓ`. The tensorial forms of `δΓ` and of `V^a` and the variation of a native family on a manifold are not stated.

**Proof status.** Proved and built. [Coefficient formulas](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/CoordinateCurvature.lean) · [Families](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/CoordinateFamily.lean) · [Palatini](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/PalatiniAlgebra.lean) · [Pointwise variation](../ProjectAinos/SmoothGeometry/Lorentzian/ScalarCurvatureVariation.lean)

## GR11. The action is relative, and its variations are admissible on one interval

**Observation.** The Einstein–Hilbert action of an open set `U` need not converge, for instance for the Minkowski metric with `Λ ≠ 0` on all of `ℝ⁴`. The difference of the densities of `g + εh` and of `g` vanishes off the support of `h`, which is compact, so its integral converges. A form `g + εh` keeps the Lorentz signature at a point for `ε` small, but how small depends on the point; the derivative under the integral needs one interval of `ε` for all points of the support.

**Interface decision.** `EinsteinHilbert.relativeAction g h κ Λ ε` is `(1/2κ) ∫ ((R_ε − 2Λ)√|g_ε| − (R − 2Λ)√|g|) d⁴x`, defined for every `ε` by the coefficient formulas, and `eventually_relativeAction_integrand` says that near `ε = 0` it is the integral of the difference of the native Lagrangians. `Minkowski.exists_lorentzFrame_of_near` keeps the signature of a bilinear form `B` with `|B(u, u) − B₀(u, u)| ≤ δ ‖u‖²`, `δ ‖e₀⁻¹‖² < 1`, where `B₀ = η(e₀ ·, e₀ ·)`; continuity of `h` and the tube lemma give one `δ` on the compact support (`CoordinateMetric.eventually_isAdmissible`). The bound uses only the values of the quadratic forms, because instance search fails for the norm of spaces `E →L[ℝ] E →L[ℝ] ℝ` in several places (G04). The derivative under the integral is taken on a closed interval of admissible `ε`, with the bound of the derivative in `ε` on the compact set `[−δ, δ] × supp h`.

**Proof status.** Proved and built.
